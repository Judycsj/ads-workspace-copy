## 2.4 流量策略和广告计费/Traffic Strategy and Ad Billing

### 2.4.1 Pacing/预算分配/Pacing/Budget Allocation

#### 2.4.1.1 定义与定位/Definition and Positioning

Pacing (budget smoothing) is a **platform-level** mechanism that controls the rate at which ad budgets are consumed, ensuring that each ad's budget is distributed at a reasonable pace throughout the delivery cycle to maximize overall platform revenue and advertiser value. Leading ad platforms such as LinkedIn, Meta, Google, and eBay all treat pacing as a core component of their ad delivery systems.

**Relationship with Bid Adjustment ([§2.3.3](03.bidding-products-and-algorithms.md#233-bid-adjustment-technology)):**


| Dimension | Bid Adjustment (λ control) | Pacing (Budget Smoothing) |
| -------- | ------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| **Perspective** | Individual advertiser / ad campaign | Platform-wide global |
| **Goal** | Cost achievement (CPA/ROAS) or full budget spend | Smooth budget consumption over time; globally optimal bidding efficiency |
| **Control Target** | Bid coefficient λ | Consumption rate (pass-through rate / pacing multiplier / deboost) |
| **Coordination** | λ determines "how much to bid" | Pacing determines "when to bid" or "how aggressively to participate in auctions" |


The two work in tandem: pacing controls the macro-level rhythm of traffic acquisition, while bid adjustment optimizes the bid level for each individual auction at the micro level.

#### 2.4.1.2 核心问题：预算撞线（Budget Hitting）/Core Problem: Budget Hitting

- **Phenomenon**: High budget consumption rates during early morning hours (present for both large and small budgets)
- **Consequence**: High-quality ads exhaust their budgets too early, causing the ad candidate pool to shrink starting from the early morning hours, bidding intensity continuously weakens, and ads miss the high-value traffic during prime-time evening hours

**Dual Negative Impact:**


| Impact Level | Specific Problem |
| -------- | ----------------------------------- |
| **Advertiser Side** | Unable to reach users during prime-time evening hours (the peak conversion window for most ads), resulting in actual advv lower than the potential optimum |
| **Platform Side** | Insufficient bidding depth during prime-time leads to CPC decline; overall revenue falls short of the optimum |


**Chain Reaction from Budget Hitting:** When large numbers of ads exhaust their budgets during the early morning and midday hours, the bidding pool shrinks sharply during the afternoon and prime-time evening. Reduced bidding depth directly compresses CPC (in a second-price auction, fewer competitors mean a lower second-highest price), so even if the remaining ads win more impressions, the per-click revenue drops significantly. For advertisers, "budget hitting" means missing the highest-converting period of the day — equivalent to spending budget that could have captured high-value traffic on low-value traffic instead.

#### 2.4.1.3 业内 Pacing 方法分类/Industry Pacing Method Classification

Industry pacing solutions can be grouped into four main approaches. The key differences lie in **how the control coefficient is defined** and **how it influences ranking and billing**:


| Method | Control Coefficient | Effect on Ranking | Effect on Billing | Representative Systems |
| ------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Hard Throttling** | Participation probability `p` (pass-through rate, PTR), `p ∈ [0, 1]` | Requests that do not participate receive a ranking score of 0 (removed from the candidate pool) | No impact (non-participating ads are not billed) | LinkedIn Pacing (KDD 2014) |
| **Rank Deboost** | Ranking pacing coef `α`, applied to the eCPM ranking score | `rank_score = α × ecpm + porg`; when α < 1, the ranking score decreases | **Does not affect billing** (the deduction formula still uses the original eCPM) | Current approach |
| **Bid Shading** | Bid pacing coef `β`, multiplied directly by the bid | `rank_score ∝ β × bid × pCTR`; ranking score decreases proportionally with β | **Affects billing** (deductions are based on the adjusted bid, lowering the advertiser's actual CPC) | Meta Mystique (WWW 2024), eBay AdaptivePacing (WWW 2024) |
| **Lambda Integration** | Pacing signal merged into the bid adjustment factor λ | Influences ranking uniformly through λ (λ is itself a bid multiplier) | **Affects billing** (deductions after λ adjustment are based on the adjusted bid) | Unified with [§2.3.3](03.bidding-products-and-algorithms.md#233-bid-adjustment-technology) bid adjustment |


**Detailed Description of the Four Methods:**

**1) Hard Throttling (Probabilistic Filtering)**

For each auction, the ad decides whether to participate with probability `p` (PTR). If consumption is too fast, PTR is reduced; if too slow, PTR is increased.

- **Control coefficient**: `p ∈ [0, 1]`, implemented as Bernoulli sampling `participate = Bernoulli(p)`
- **Ranking**: Non-participating requests are removed directly from the candidate pool and do not enter ranking
- **Billing**: Fully decoupled from billing — when participating, normal bid and GSP/GFP rules apply; no billing when not participating
- **Pros**: Simplest implementation; fully decoupled from the bidding and ranking systems
- **Cons**: Treats high-value and low-value requests equally; may filter out auction opportunities that could have converted efficiently

**2) Rank Deboost**

The pacing coefficient acts only on the ranking score without changing the bid or billing logic.

- **Control coefficient**: Ranking pacing coef `α`, applied to the eCPM ranking score in pre-ranking and mixed ranking
- **Ranking**: `rank_score = α × ecpm_weight × ecpm + porg`; when `α < 1`, the ad's ranking competitiveness decreases and win probability is reduced
- **Billing**: **No impact** — the deduction formula uses the original eCPM (excluding α); the advertiser's actual CPC is unaffected by pacing
- **Pros**: Fully decoupled from the billing system; does not alter the advertiser's bidding or billing experience
- **Cons**: Indirect control effect — reducing the ranking score with α does not linearly reduce the win probability; the effect depends on the competitive landscape

**3) Bid Shading**

The pacing coefficient is multiplied directly by the ad bid, affecting both ranking and billing simultaneously.

- **Control coefficient**: Bid pacing coef `β`; `adjusted_bid = β × original_bid`
- **Ranking**: `rank_score ∝ β × bid × pCTR`; ranking score changes proportionally with β
- **Billing**: **Affects billing** — GSP deductions are based on the adjusted bid ranking; the advertiser's actual CPC decreases as β decreases
- **Pros**: Ranking and billing are consistent; the control effect is direct and predictable
- **Cons**: Changes the advertiser's actual billing; requires careful interaction design with bid adjustment λ to avoid dual-control conflicts

**4) Lambda Integration (Unified with Bid Adjustment)**

The pacing signal is incorporated directly into the bid adjustment factor λ ([§2.3.3](03.bidding-products-and-algorithms.md#233-bid-adjustment-technology)); no separate pacing coefficient is maintained.

- **Control coefficient**: λ simultaneously carries both cost/ROI control and budget pacing objectives
- **Ranking**: Bid is adjusted via λ, indirectly affecting ranking
- **Billing**: **Affects billing** — similar to Bid Shading; the λ-adjusted bid participates in deduction calculation
- **Pros**: Simplest system architecture; no additional pacing module needed; budget pacing and cost control are optimized jointly
- **Cons**: λ must simultaneously handle two objectives — cost achievement and budget smoothing — resulting in high control complexity (see [§2.3.3.1.3](03.bidding-products-and-algorithms.md#23313-mcb-budget-spend--roi-lower-bound-constraint) dual-constraint problem)

**Real-time Control Algorithms:** The control coefficients (p / α / β / λ) for all four methods can be updated in real time using PID or MPC algorithms:

- **PID Controller**: Adjusts the coefficient based on the deviation (error signal) between actual spend and target spend, using proportional-integral-derivative feedback. Reactive control; simple to implement
- **MPC Controller**: Uses a Bid2X model to predict future consumption trends under different coefficient values, then selects the optimal coefficient that balances spending speed against bid target constraints (e.g., excluding coefficient values that would cause the budget to hit before a specified time)

#### 2.4.1.4 目标消耗曲线与实时调控/Target Spending Curve and Real-Time Control

The complete pacing workflow consists of two steps: **offline determination of the target spending curve** (what pace the budget should be spent at) and **online real-time control** (adjusting the coefficients from [§2.4.1.3](#2413-industry-pacing-method-classification) so that actual spend tracks the target curve).

**I. Target Spending Curve Design**


| Strategy | Description | Applicable Scenario |
| ----------------------------------- | --------------------------- | ------------------ |
| **Even Pacing** | Budget distributed evenly over time (e.g., daily budget / 24 hours) | Brand awareness ads with no time-specific conversion focus |
| **Traffic-weighted Pacing** | Budget distributed proportional to historical traffic value, allocating more budget to high-value time slots | Performance ads focused on maximizing GMV/conversions |


Key design points for a traffic-weighted pacing curve:

- Compute a **traffic value index** for each time slot from historical data (combining GMV, conversion rate, CPC, and other metrics)
- Identify and protect **prime-time evening characteristics** (typically 18:00–22:00), where conversion rate and GMV share are significantly higher than other time slots
- The curve typically uses 30-minute or hourly granularity and is configured differently by region and category

**II. Online Real-Time Control**

Once the target spending curve is established, the online layer monitors the deviation `error(t) = actual_spend(t) - target_spend(t)` at a minute-level frequency and updates the control coefficients (p / α / β / λ) from [§2.4.1.3](#2413-industry-pacing-method-classification) in real time using PID or MPC algorithms:


| Control Algorithm | How to Update the Coefficient | Applicable Methods |
| ------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| **PID** | Proportional-integral-derivative feedback based on current deviation: `error > 0` (spending too fast) → reduce coefficient to suppress consumption; `error < 0` → increase coefficient to accelerate consumption | Applicable to all four methods; simple to implement |
| **MPC** | Uses the Bid2X model ([§2.3.4](03.bidding-products-and-algorithms.md#234-bid2x-mpc-prediction-model)) to predict future consumption trajectories under different coefficient values; selects the coefficient that maximizes delivery effectiveness subject to the constraint that "the budget is fully spent by the target time" | Primarily for Bid Shading and Lambda Integration |


**Specific Control Differences Across Methods:**

- **Hard Throttling + PID**: PID outputs PTR increments/decrements; `p(t+1) = clip(p(t) + Δp, 0, 1)`. Control granularity is coarse (participate/not-participate is a binary decision), but implementation is the simplest
- **Rank Deboost + PID**: PID outputs α increments/decrements; α acts on the ranking score. Because the relationship between α and win probability is nonlinear (depending on the competitive landscape), PID parameters must be tuned separately for different bidding depths
- **Bid Shading + PID/MPC**: PID or MPC outputs the β value; β is multiplied directly by the bid. Because β affects both ranking and billing simultaneously, the control effect is more direct and predictable; MPC can use the Bid2X model to precisely predict consumption trajectories under different β values
- **λ Integration + MPC**: λ carries both cost control and budget pacing objectives; when MPC searches for the optimal λ, it must simultaneously satisfy cost/ROI constraints and budget smoothing constraints (see [§2.3.3.1.3](03.bidding-products-and-algorithms.md#23313-mcb-budget-spend--roi-lower-bound-constraint) dual-constraint problem)

**III. Two-Layer Architecture (Reference: Mystique)**


| Layer | Responsibility | Update Frequency |
| ------- | ------------------------------------ | ---- |
| **Offline Layer** | Generates a daily target spending curve for each ad campaign based on historical consumption data and traffic distribution | Daily |
| **Online Layer** | Monitors deviation between actual spend and target curve in real time; dynamically adjusts control coefficients via PID/MPC | Minute-level |

#### 2.4.1.6 Pacing 为何由平台控制/Why Pacing Is Platform-Controlled

Pacing is a **platform-level** rather than **advertiser-level** control mechanism. Currently, advertisers cannot choose whether to enable or disable Pacing. The core reasons are:

1. **Global optimum vs. individual optimum**: A single advertiser disabling Pacing could grab more cheap traffic at midnight, but if all advertisers disabled Pacing, midnight auction depth would spike while prime-time auction pools would be depleted, reducing overall platform CPC and group-level advertiser advv (see the cascading effects of budget hitting in [§2.4.1.2](#2412-核心问题预算撞线budget-hitting))
2. **Game-theoretic equilibrium**: Once Pacing becomes optional, rational advertisers would choose to disable it for short-term advantage, creating a prisoner's dilemma — the equilibrium outcome is everyone disabling it, reverting to the budget-hitting problem
3. **eCPM auction fairness**: Pacing adjusts ranking scores via ecpm_weight. If some advertisers were exempt from Pacing constraints, they would have an asymmetric advantage in auctions, undermining the fairness of the second-price mechanism

### 2.4.2 流量扶持框架/Traffic Support Framework

#### 2.4.2.1 扶持动机与业界思路/Motivation and Industry Approaches

Ad systems rely primarily on auction mechanisms to select ads from all candidates, which naturally produces a **Matthew effect**: mature ads with rich historical data are more easily retrieved at recall and more accurately estimated at ranking, while new or cold-start ads are disadvantaged at every stage due to insufficient data. Traffic support (Business Support / Traffic Boost) layers targeted intervention capabilities on top of the auction framework through manual rules and strategy adjustments to address the following problems:


| Support Scenario | Description |
| ------------ | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Cold-start ad support** | New ads lack historical data; they are easily missed at recall and have inaccurate pCTR/pCVR predictions at ranking. Support is needed to provide exposure opportunities and accumulate data |
| **Balancing advertiser interests** | Top advertisers may monopolize traffic; support queues for SMB advertisers or long-tail products prevent traffic concentration and improve advertiser retention |
| **Meeting specific business goals** | Prioritize promotion of specific merchants (e.g., merchants migrating to ROI2.0, new merchants, merchants with low budget utilization) |


**Common industry approaches** can be summarized along three dimensions:


| Dimension | Core Idea | Typical Methods |
| ------- | ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Model side** | Improve model estimation accuracy for disadvantaged ads | Prediction calibration (isotonic regression), reducing ID-based features and increasing generalization features (statistical features, embeddings, real-time features), Exploration & Exploitation (Bandit / RL), transfer learning for long-tail ads |
| **Mechanism side** | Expand volume + adjust ranking, trading short-term revenue/efficiency loss for support goal achievement | Separate recall channels, Rank Boost, Quota guarantees, traffic quotas (minimum impression share per advertiser/category), tiered traffic amplification, entry/exit mechanisms |
| **Product side** | Lower competitive thresholds for target ads | Dedicated ad slots, one-click volume boost tools, targeted traffic packages, business-specific event placements |


**Traffic control technology evolution** (referencing content platform practices; the approach equally applies to ad support scenarios):

```
Forced insertion → Boost (upweighting) → Volume guarantee → Differentiated volume guarantee
```

- **Forced insertion**: The most primitive method; target ads/content are inserted directly into results; poor user experience
- **Boost (upweighting)**: Multiplies the ranking score of target ads in pre-ranking/re-ranking by a coefficient > 1; simple to implement but impression volume is sensitive to the coefficient value
- **Volume guarantee**: Sets a guarantee target for each target ad (e.g., 100 impressions within 24 hours); dynamically adjusts the boost coefficient based on the gap between current impressions and the target
- **Differentiated volume guarantee**: Sets different guarantee targets for different ads based on signals such as ad quality and advertiser value; once the target is met, support stops and the ad returns to natural competition

This evolution from coarse-grained to fine-grained approaches applies not only to cold start but also to other support scenarios (e.g., guaranteeing volume for low-budget-utilization ads, differentially allocating traffic for specific business ads).

**Cost and trade-offs of support**: **Any form of support is essentially a trade-off between short-term revenue/efficiency loss and long-term ecosystem health.** Academic research shows that well-designed cold-start algorithms can improve success rates by more than 60% with a short-term revenue loss of less than 1% (see [§2.4.2.8](#2428-key-references)). The core optimization objective is **to achieve the best support effect with the minimum revenue/efficiency loss** — whether measured by cold-start success rate, advertiser retention rate, or achievement of specific business goals.

#### 2.4.2.2 全链路扶持框架设计/Full-Funnel Support Framework Design

**Design Elements of the Support Framework**

Early support systems often had each business team adding ad-hoc intervention logic scattered across various modules in the delivery pipeline. As the number of business use cases grew, significant shortcomings emerged in areas such as candidate selection capability, strategy granularity, code reuse, and effectiveness measurement. A good support framework should have the following design elements:


| Design Element | Description |
| ---------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Flexible candidate selection** | Supports not only offline labeling but also dynamically deciding at runtime — based on pipeline context (recall channel, user real-time features) — whether a candidate needs support; audience/query selection rules are configurable; adding new rules requires no new field or operator development |
| **Fine-grained weight adjustment** | Goes beyond simple upweighting/downweighting; provides general strategy operators (quota guarantee, bid weight adjustment, dynamic forced insertion) and custom plugin interfaces; supports fine-grained traffic selection strategies such as dynamic bid support during the mixed ranking stage |
| **Unified full-funnel management** | All manual intervention strategies are bound to a unified business queue (Tag); the scope and specific parameters of each strategy are controlled uniformly through the queue tagging logic, avoiding repeated development of similar intervention logic across different business teams in each module |
| **Agile onboarding for new businesses** | Most business requirements can be fulfilled using configurable base operators (selection, tagging, weight adjustment, quota guarantee, bidding) with zero code development; complex requirements are handled via a custom operator plugin interface |
| **Measurable support cost** | Can quantify the revenue/efficiency loss (e.g., eCPM decline, CTR dilution) for each support business, rather than relying solely on post-hoc A/B experiment evaluation |
| **Measurable support effect/ROI** | Provides per-business-queue routine monitoring of effectiveness (impression share, penetration rate, posterior XTR, funnel-stage dropout rate and candidate share, etc.); makes the impact surface and effectiveness of each business queue on the overall market transparent |
| **Budget allocation to maximize ROI** | Within a limited support budget (traffic share), automatically optimizes quota allocation across business queues to maximize overall support ROI |
| **Funnel monitoring and attribution** | Business queue candidate statistics at each truncated funnel stage (average item count, proportion of requests with candidate count >= 3/5/10/30, proportion of requests where queue candidates account for >= 1%/3%/5%/10%/30%, etc.); supports rapid identification of support failure points; enables timely detection of negative effects when strategy code becomes stale |


Based on these design elements, the core design philosophy of our full-funnel support framework is: **all manual strategies are mounted onto a unified Tag system, driven by configuration, with the engineering positioning as a general-purpose strategy engine.** The framework supports coexistence of multiple business use cases; new businesses can go live with a small traffic slice within 2 business days.

```
                         +------------------------------+
                         |        Exit Mechanism        |
                         | (Data sufficient / Target met|
                         |     / Budget exhausted)      |

                                       ^
                                       |
             +---> Budget Allocation --+----> Bid Adjustment
             |              ^                        |
             |              |                        v
Candidate ---+    Billing / Cost  <--- Win  <--- Rank Boost /
 Tagging     |     Calculation       Auction      MixRank
             |                                       ^

                      Recall          Guarantee
```

#### 2.4.2.3 候选圈选与打标/Candidate Selection and Tagging

The support framework implements fine-grained candidate selection through a two-layer Tag system:

**Item Tag (offline labeling):**

- Different business use cases correspond to different candidate pools; items in a pool are labeled with the same item_tag
- A Tag consists of **64 bits**; each bit represents one business use case, and a value of 1 for a given bit indicates that the corresponding business is active
- Data flow: business team provides candidate items → written to a unified tag table → synchronized to Redis → updated to the ads info forward index via Tag Service

**Biz Tag (online computation):**

- To enable **targeted support** (effective only for specific audiences/queries), the biz_tag is computed in real time based on user features and query features
- Also 64 bits; each bit represents one audience/query category
- Depends on user profiles (gender, age, click count, purchase count, behavior sequences) and query understanding features from the FSE feature table

**Tag combination logic**: During an online request, support is activated only when both `item_tag AND biz_tag` have a value of 1 for the corresponding bit — i.e., the item must both "belong to the support candidate set" and "the user/query must belong to the target audience."

#### 2.4.2.4 分库召回/Separate-Index Recall

An **independent recall queue** (separate item index) is established for each support business use case; TopK candidates are retrieved separately from this index for each request, preventing support candidates from being drowned out in general recall. Based on the underlying recall channel type, there are three implementation categories:


| Recall Type | Implementation | Applicable Scenario |
| ---------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------- |
| **KV-based recall** | Offline: filter the inverted index chains of seed items by tag, retain results matching the tag, generate a sub-queue and write to Redis | Statistical recall methods such as itemCF |
| **Offline vector recall** | Offline: filter candidate embeddings by tag, recompute I2I / U2I / Q2I on the filtered subset, and take TopK | deep I2I, offline Q2I, etc. |
| **Online vector recall** | Online: filter by tag during Vespa HNSW retrieval, selecting neighbors matching the tag at each retrieval layer | online Q2I, online U2I |


Sub-queues from multiple business use cases are merged into a unified shadow queue via **zigzag merging**; online retrieval uses `model_name` to distinguish between different queues.

#### 2.4.2.5 全链路统一调权/Unified Full-Funnel Weight Adjustment

After support candidates pass through recall, they receive unified weight adjustment at every subsequent funnel stage:

**Step 1 — Business Queue Tagging (Recall → Prerank):**

When zigzag merges multi-channel recall results, it simultaneously tags candidates with biz_tag. After tagging, the candidates form **multiple business queues** (with tag labels) and **one non-business queue**, which are all fed into pre-ranking together.

**Step 2 — Quota Guarantee (Prerank → Rank):**

After pre-ranking scoring is complete, for business queues that require quota guarantee, the system identifies candidates by `biz_tag` and selects TopK from the business queue according to `reserveQuotaRatio` (a configured ratio, typically 5%–10%), then **force-inserts them at the top of the candidate results** and sends them directly to the Rank stage. Guaranteed candidates are exempt from zigzag truncation and other strategy filters after pre-ranking.

**Step 3 — Rank Boost Weight Adjustment (Rank → MixRank):**

In the Rank stage, item_tag is passed in, and the corresponding business configuration (e.g., `pidBasedCoef`, `ruleBasedCoef`) is read; the ranking score of support candidates is adjusted accordingly. The adjusted ranking re-sorts candidates to ensure support candidates rank higher.

**Relationship with billing**: `rank_boost` contributes to the ranking score calculation but **does not participate in billing** — in the billing formula in [§2.4.3.1](#2431-product-ads), `rank_boost` is explicitly deducted:

```
deduction_price[i] = (rank_score[i+1] - porg[i] - ecpm_weight[i] × rank_boost[i])
                     / (ecpm_weight[i] × pctr[i])
```

#### 2.4.2.6 退出机制/Exit Mechanism

Support is not permanent; appropriate exit conditions must be designed to avoid over-intervention:


| Exit Condition | Description |
| ---------- | ----------------------------------- |
| **Cold start complete** | Ad exits the support queue after accumulating sufficient impression/click/conversion data and returns to natural competition |
| **Business goal met** | E.g., an ad with low budget utilization exits after K days of support if budget utilization meets the target (e.g., > 50%) |
| **Support budget exhausted** | Downstream pipeline stops support (recall side may still retain candidates, resulting in a small amount of quota waste) |
| **Gradual exit** | Progressively reduce the boost coefficient rather than stopping abruptly, to avoid cliff-like traffic changes that cause data degradation |


Risk of over-support: if the boost is too strong, ads will be shown to mismatched audiences, causing abnormally low CTR/CVR, and after cold start the ranking model may suppress them for an extended period. Therefore, the support intensity must strike a balance between "sufficient exposure" and "precise matching."

#### 2.4.2.7 策略配置与监控/Strategy Configuration and Monitoring

The framework configures the support strategy for each business via **A/B parameters** prefixed with the business name (e.g., `coldStart_`), supporting coexistence of multiple business use cases:


| Parameter | Type | Description | Scope |
| ------------------- | ----- | ------------------------------ | -------------- |
| `itemTagMask` | int | Bit position(s) used by this business (1–64), associated with item tag | Full funnel |
| `bizTagMask` | int | Bit position(s) used by this business (1–64), associated with biz tag | Full funnel |
| `reserveQuotaRatio` | float | Quota ratio for business queue promotion after pre-ranking; 0 means no promotion | Post pre-ranking |
| `pidBasedCoef` | float | Support intensity coefficient based on PID control; default 1.0 | Online Bidding |
| `ruleBasedCoef` | float | Manually configured support coefficient | Online Bidding |


**Monitoring**: Uses the tag_id corresponding to biz_tag to count the average number of candidates for each business queue at different funnel stages (recall, pre-ranking, ranking, mixed ranking), enabling evaluation of support effectiveness and funnel loss.

### 2.4.3 混排和计费/Mixed Ranking and Billing

**Coordination between Pacing and Mixed Ranking:**

Pacing ([§2.4.1](#241-pacingbudget-allocation)) and mixed ranking coordinate through the ranking score formula:

1. **Pacing controls ranking score**: The current Rank Deboost approach applies pacing coef α directly to the eCPM component of the mixed ranking score (`rank_score = α × ecpm_weight × ecpm + porg`); when α < 1, the ad's competitiveness in mixed ranking decreases
2. **Mixed ranking determines display position**: During mixed ranking, the adjusted rank_score competes with organic content for slots; lower α → lower ad ranking → reduced win rate → slower spending
3. **Billing decoupled from Pacing**: The billing formula uses the original ecpm (without α), so the advertiser's actual CPC is unaffected by pacing

Overall effect: Pacing controls "when and how aggressively to compete" on the time dimension, while mixed ranking determines "where ads appear" on the spatial dimension. The two systems connect through rank_score — Pacing adjusts the ranking score intensity, and mixed ranking allocates slots based on the adjusted ranking score.

**Differences by ad type:**

The billing model, billing trigger point, and mixed ranking optimization objective differ significantly across ad types:


| Dimension | Product Ads | Brand & Shop Ads | Video Ads | Live Ads |
| ---------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| **Billing model** | GSP (multi-slot auction) | GFP (single-slot auction) | GSP (multi-slot auction) | FP (with rankcap & bidcap introduced for second-price discounting) |
| **Billing trigger** | Click | Click | Mingtou: Impression; Antou: Click | Mingtou: Impression; Antou: Click |
| **Mixed ranking objective** | Rcmd: order/gmv/rev/pc2; Search: order/gmv/relevance/rev/pc2 | No mixed ranking (delivery pipeline fully controlled by the ads side) | Some scenarios (e.g., ID trending): rev/order/gmv/pc2/watchtime; most other scenarios: forced insertion | rev/order/gmv/pc2/watchtime/follow/comment/like |


#### 2.4.3.1 商品广告/Product Ads

Product Ads use the **uGSP (Unified Generalized Second Price)** settlement mechanism, which builds on GSP by incorporating a quality score that takes into account ad eCPM, the recommendation side's pGMV, relevance, and other information. The GMV Max product's ads-organic traffic integration is achieved through API0-API3 interactions; see [§2.3.5](03.bidding-products-and-algorithms.md#235-gmv-max-auto-bidding) for details.

**Unified billing across traffic entry points:** Product Ads cover both Search and RCMD traffic types. RCMD includes entry groups such as DD (Daily Discover), YMAL (You May Also Like), PP (Promoted Placement), GAME, and IN_SHOP (in-shop page recommendations). All Product Ads entry groups share the same uGSP deduction logic and click-based billing — there is no billing difference across traffic entry points.

**Ranking score formula:**

```
ads rank_score = ecpm_weight × (ecpm + rank_boost) + porg
```

- `ecpm_weight`: eCPM weight (i.e., alpha in the mixed ranking formula)
- `ecpm = cpc_bid × pCTR`
- `rank_boost`: support term from the ads side
- `porg`: non-eCPM score (recommendation/relevance), coefficient fixed at 1 (i.e., beta = 1 in the mixed ranking formula)

**ecpm_weight configuration and tuning:**

In practice, the porg coefficient (beta) is fixed at 1; only `ecpm_weight` (alpha) is tuned. `ecpm_weight` is dynamically adjusted via PID control based on the ads-free group's GMV loss, ensuring that the user experience degradation caused by ads remains controllable. The control operates in two time-window layers:

1. **Long window (daily)**: Determines the target adload. Since GMV loss fluctuates significantly, the short-cycle PID controls adload rather than GMV loss directly; the long cycle uses GMV loss data under different adload levels to determine the target adload that keeps GMV loss within the acceptable range
2. **Short window (minute-level)**: PID feedback adjusts real-time adload to track the target adload determined by the long window

In essence, a larger `ecpm_weight` increases ad competitiveness in mixed ranking → higher adload → greater GMV loss; a smaller `ecpm_weight` reduces ad competitiveness → lower adload → less GMV loss. PID dynamically adjusts `ecpm_weight` to balance revenue against user experience.

**Billing formula (click-based billing):**

```
deduction_price[i] = (rank_score[i+1] - porg[i] - ecpm_weight[i] × rank_boost[i]) / (ecpm_weight[i] × pctr[i])
```

**Additional deduction parameter fields in ranking and billing:**

The online-bidding rerank stage outputs three Additional-series fields to ads-engine's deduction stage via `DeductionParam`. The `rank_boost` in the formulas above corresponds to `AdditionalBoost` in the engineering implementation:

| DeductionParam Field | Meaning | Impact on Ranking | Impact on Billing |
|---------------------|---------|-------------------|-------------------|
| `AdditionalBoost` | Extra ranking boost (e.g., voucher profit, subsidy boost) | Participates in final ranking: `final_rank = ecpm + AdditionalBoost`, improving display position | **Not billed** — explicitly deducted in the billing formula |
| `AdditionalDeboost` | Extra ranking suppression (e.g., over-delivery deboost, pacing deboost) | Reduces ranking competitiveness | **Not billed** — same principle as Rank Deboost |
| `AdditionalDeductionPrice` | Direct additive surcharge to billing (absolute value, CPC unit) | Does not affect ranking | **Directly billed** — added to deduction_price in CPC mode; multiplied by pctr then added in oCPM mode |

ads-engine's two-pass sorting mechanism:
1. First sort (pure ecpm, without boost) → records `NextRankScoreWithoutBoost` for second-price billing reference
2. Second sort (ecpm + AdditionalBoost) → determines the final display order

Therefore, the ad display order on the page and the second-price billing reference score are not the same value.

Relationship with `pOrg`: `pOrg` is the recommendation-side non-eCPM score (organic score), independent of `AdditionalBoost` in both ranking and billing — rank_score = ecpm_weight × (ecpm + AdditionalBoost) + pOrg; during billing, both pOrg and AdditionalBoost are deducted, ensuring advertisers only pay for their eCPM competitiveness.

**Core differences between uGSP and standard GSP:**


| Dimension | Standard GSP | uGSP |
| ----- | --------- | --------------------- |
| Ranking basis | Sorted by bid only | Sorted by f(bid, quality score) |
| Payment price | Directly uses the next-ranked advertiser's bid | Next-ranked advertiser's composite score converted to the price corresponding to the current quality score |
| Objective | Maximize short-term platform revenue | Balance revenue with ad quality |
| Advertiser strategy | Optimize bid only | Simultaneously optimize bid and ad quality (CTR/CVR) |


**Deduction Price Clipping (Deduction Bounds):**

After the uGSP second-price formula computes the raw deduction price, multiple clipping layers ensure the price remains reasonable:

```
1. second_price_ratio floor: deduction_price = max(deduction_price, second_price_ratio × bid)
2. cap_deduction_price_ratio ceiling: deduction_price = min(deduction_price, cap_ratio × ugsp_price)
3. first_price_cap: deduction_price = min(deduction_price, bid × first_price_cap_coef)
4. Country-level minimum: deduction_price = max(deduction_price, country_min_delta_price)
5. Per-ad min_cap / max_cap: clip(deduction_price, DeductionPriceMinCap, DeductionPriceMaxCap)
   (falls back to AB params FallBackMinDeductionPriceCap / FallBackMaxDeductionPriceCap if 0)
```

| Clipping Layer | Purpose | Source |
|----------------|---------|--------|
| `SecondPriceRatio` | Prevents excessively low second price (floor) | online-bidding DeductionParam |
| `CapDeductionPriceRatio` | Prevents excessively high second price (ceiling, relative to raw uGSP price) | AB test parameter |
| `FirstPriceCapCoef` | First-price cap ensuring deduction does not exceed a proportion of the bid | AB test parameter |
| `country_min_delta_price` | Country-level minimum deduction to avoid extreme low prices | Country config |
| `DeductionPriceMinCap/MaxCap` | Per-ad floor/ceiling | online-bidding sets per ad |

The OCPM path applies an additional ceiling of `DeductionPriceMaxCap × OcpmMaxCapPctr`, preventing OCPM deduction from exceeding the equivalent CPC ceiling in low-conversion scenarios.

#### 2.4.3.2 店铺广告/Shop Ads

Shop Ads use **GFP (Generalized First Price, single-slot auction)** settlement. The delivery pipeline is fully controlled by the ads side (Recall → Relevance Filter → Ranking → Rerank → Send); **no mixed ranking**. Billing trigger: Click.

#### 2.4.3.3 视频广告/Video Ads

Video Ads use **GSP (multi-slot auction)** settlement:

- **Mingtou**: Billing trigger is Impression
- **Antou**: Billing trigger is Click
- Mixed ranking exists only in some scenarios (e.g., ID trending); optimization objectives include rev/order/gmv/pc2/watchtime (requiring conversion between eCPM and non-revenue/non-GMV objectives)
- Most other regions and scenarios currently use **forced insertion** (Enforced Placement — ads are placed directly at a specified position regardless of their organic ranking)

#### 2.4.3.4 直播广告/Live Ads

Live Ads use **FP (First Price)** billing, with rankcap & bidcap introduced to apply a second-price discount (achieving an effect similar to second-price):

- **Mingtou**: Billing trigger is Impression
- **Antou**: Billing trigger is Click
- Mixed ranking optimization objectives: rev/order/gmv/pc2/watchtime/follow/comment/like (requiring conversion between eCPM and non-revenue, non-GMV objectives)

#### 2.4.3.5 超投防控/Over-Delivery Prevention

Over-Delivery occurs when the remaining ad budget is insufficient to cover the last deduction, causing `expected_rev > real_rev` and resulting in platform revenue loss (rev_loss). Over-delivery prevention spans the full pipeline from recall to billing:

| Stage | Mechanism | Description |
|-------|-----------|-------------|
| **Recall** | `OVERDELIVERYANTIFRAUDFILTER` | Filters high-risk ads based on anti-fraud policies at the recall stage; see [§3.3](../03.ads-engine/03.ads-recall-service.md) |
| **API2 (Online Bidding)** | Over-delivery risk detection + deboost + additional_deduction | online-bidding compares `cpc_bid` with `rt_remain_budget`; for at-risk ads, it reduces the ranking score (deboost) and computes `additional_deduction` to lower the deduction price; see [§2.3.3.6](03.bidding-products-and-algorithms.md#2336-product-ads-over-invest-control) |
| **API3 (Deduction)** | Deduction bounds | Deduction clipping layers (min_cap/max_cap/first_price_cap) serve as guardrails against extreme deductions; see the deduction price clipping section above |

#### 2.4.3.6 oCPM 与 oCPC 的计费差异/oCPM vs oCPC Billing Differences

oCPM (Optimized CPM) and oCPC (Optimized CPC) are **identical** in bidding strategy, ranking, reserve price, over-delivery control, and boost mechanisms. The only difference is the **unit of measurement in the deduction formula**:

| Dimension | oCPC | oCPM |
|-----------|------|------|
| **Billing trigger** | Click | Impression |
| **Deduction formula** | `deduction_price = (NextRankScore - Porg) / (EcpmWeight × Pctr)` | `deduction_price = (NextRankScore - Porg) / EcpmWeight` |
| **Unit** | Per-click price (CPC) | Per-mille impression price (CPM), no division by Pctr |
| **AdditionalDeductionPrice** | Added directly to deduction_price | Multiplied by Pctr then added (unit alignment) |
| **Capping** | `DeductionPriceMaxCap` | `DeductionPriceMaxCap × OcpmMaxCapPctr` (additional Pctr calibration) |

**No differences in other mechanisms**: Second-price rate calculation, deduction_reason classification, reserve price (`ReservePriceBeta/Tr`), over-delivery detection and deboost, and boost logic are all identical between oCPM and oCPC.

**Unit conversion**: oCPM deduction price ≈ oCPC deduction price × Pctr × 1000. That is, the per-impression deduction under oCPM = the per-click deduction under oCPC × click-through rate.

#### 2.4.3.7 预算撞线与 Dormant 机制/Budget Exhaustion and Dormant Mechanism

Dormant is a special state for ads that have exhausted their budget: for ads that have **hit budget AND are over-collected** (actual advv below expected level), the system keeps them online instead of taking them offline, allowing them to continue accumulating attributable ad value (advv) and thereby improving the advertiser's campaign performance.

**Core logic**: When a budget-exhausted ad has over-collected (actual advv < expected level), continued display accumulates more attribution conversions (such as orders and GMV), helping to improve the advertiser's ROI performance without incurring additional charges.

**Dormant handling across modules:**

| Module | Behavior | Description |
|--------|----------|-------------|
| **Recall** | `AdsDormantConversionCheck` | Ad visibility check: for Search, DD/YMAL/PP entry points, dormant ads are checked against the TagB dormant conversion tag — matched ads are allowed to compete; unmatched ads are filtered (Unpick Reason: `OfflineFilterAdsDormantConversion`). See [§2.1](01.recall-and-supply-strategy.md) AdsInfoFilter |
| **Bidding** | Soft offline / near-zero coef | In GmvMax scenarios, when `FinalRemainBudget <= 0`, `CoefTotal` is set to `1e-6` (near-zero bid), marked as `hit_budget_cap`. See [§2.3.3](03.bidding-products-and-algorithms.md#233-bidding-control-techniques) |
| **Boost** | Dormant campaign exclusion | boost-support-service applies three conditions for dormant campaign exclusion: (1) all ads have dormantTagBit=64; (2) `visible_start_ts` is after campaign start; (3) `visible_start_ts` is in the future. Only when all three are met is the campaign excluded; otherwise it still participates in weight calculation |
| **Billing** | No deduction | Dormant ad impressions do not incur deductions (budget is exhausted); they serve solely for attribution conversion accumulation |
| **Voucher** | `deduction_reason = 1` | Budget-exhausted dormant ads are identified via `deduction_reason = 1`, distinguishing them from regular ads voucher issuance and non-ads voucher issuance (`pricing_type = 29`). See [§2.5](05.ads-smart-voucher.md) |
| **Adsload** | Not in auction | Budget-exhausted ads do not participate in normal auctions, reducing actual ad density |
