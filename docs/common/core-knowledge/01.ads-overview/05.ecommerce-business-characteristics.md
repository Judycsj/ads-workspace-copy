## 1.5 电商广告的业务特性与产品创新 / Business Characteristics and Product Innovation in E-commerce Advertising

### 1.5.1 电商广告业务特性 / E-Commerce Advertising Business Characteristics

<!-- last_translated: 2026-06-05T09:27:40.090692+00:00 -->

Although e-commerce advertising and generic advertising (brand display ads, feed ads, etc.) share the basic framework of "auction, delivery, attribution," the two differ fundamentally along several core dimensions due to the inherent business characteristics of e-commerce platforms. Understanding these differences helps explain why Shopee Paid Ads' product design and technical solutions are structured the way they are.

| Dimension | E-Commerce Advertising | Generic Advertising (Brand/Feed) |
| ---------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| **Distance from transaction** | Directly embedded in the shopping context (search results, product recommendation pages); the "see ad → place order" path is very short | More of an "exposure → awareness → subsequent conversion" flow; attribution path is long and blurry |
| **Measurability of performance** | The platform natively owns the closed-loop transaction data; ad performance can be precisely attributed to GMV/ROAS | Performance (brand awareness lift, offline store visits, etc.) is difficult to directly quantify |
| **Supply scale and dynamism** | Must handle millions of SKUs, with inventory, prices, and promotional status changing in real time | "Creatives" are relatively stable and do not go on/off-shelf as frequently as SKUs |
| **Traffic seasonality** | Traffic is highly concentrated during major promotional events (Double 11, mid-year sales); traffic and budget can surge several times over | Seasonal fluctuations are relatively mild |
| **Competition between organic and paid traffic** | The platform simultaneously owns organic recommendation traffic and ad traffic, both sharing the same pool of users and products (see [§1.4.6](04.strategy-mechanisms.md#146-广告混排) Blending); complex traffic allocation dynamics exist | Independent DSPs and similar platforms do not have organic traffic; this competition does not exist |
| **Advertiser ecosystem** | Advertisers are predominantly long-tail small and medium-sized merchants (SMEs) who lack professional campaign teams and experience, and are sensitive to operational complexity | Advertisers are primarily brands/agencies with professional campaign teams who can handle complex campaign configurations |

These differences directly influence the technical choices and product form of e-commerce advertising systems:

- **Close to transaction + measurable performance** → oCPX ([§1.4.3.2](04.strategy-mechanisms.md#1432-智能出价-smart-bidding-ocpx)) and other conversion-performance bidding modes first achieved large-scale adoption in e-commerce advertising, because the platform can precisely measure every conversion
- **Massive and dynamically changing SKUs** → the recall and ranking systems must handle real-time changes in the product pool (insufficient inventory, price changes, promotional on/off-shelving) — encountered far less frequently in generic advertising
- **Promotion-driven** → bid control ([§1.4.3.3](04.strategy-mechanisms.md#1433-λ-调价系数)) and budget pacing must handle extreme scenarios where traffic multiplies several times during major events
- **Competition between organic and paid traffic** → gave rise to products like full-site promotion ([§1.5.2](#152-全站推广)) that jointly optimize paid and organic traffic
- **Long-tail advertisers + lack of campaign experience** → product design must move toward automation and managed services: from manual CPC to oCPX ([§1.4.3.2](04.strategy-mechanisms.md#1432-智能出价-smart-bidding-ocpx)) to full-site promotion ([§1.5.2](#152-全站推广)), each step lowering the operational barrier for advertisers
- **Measurable performance + closed-loop transactions** → the platform can precisely calculate the ROI of subsidies, giving rise to products like QCPX ([§1.5.3](#153-qcpx-智能优惠券出价)) where the platform itself funds subsidies to boost conversions — something almost impossible to achieve in brand advertising where performance is hard to quantify

### 1.5.2 全站推广 / Full-Site Promotion

#### 1.5.2.1 什么是全站推广 / What Is Full-Site Promotion

<!-- last_translated: 2026-06-05T09:28:01.577897+00:00 -->

Full-site Promotion is an advertising product form in which the advertising platform **jointly optimizes paid traffic and organic traffic**. Advertisers only need to set a budget (+ an optional ROI target), and the platform allocates paid and organic impressions from the full-site traffic pool (search + recommendation + all channels) to maximize the advertiser's overall GMV.

Core differences between full-site promotion and traditional advertising:


| Dimension | Traditional Advertising (Independent Pipeline) | Full-Site Promotion (Integrated Pipeline) |
| ----- | --------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| Traffic scope | Advertisers purchase ad slots; organic traffic is independently allocated | Platform allocates impressions jointly from paid + organic traffic pool |
| Advertiser sets | Bid (CPC/CPA/ROAS) + budget + targeting | Budget + optional ROI target (NoBid or tROAS) |
| System architecture | Ads are independently recalled and ranked; inserted into organic results at the final stage ([§1.4.6.2](04.strategy-mechanisms.md#1462-独立链路-vs-融合链路)) | Ads and organic content are recalled and ranked together ([§1.3.1](03.system-pipeline-modules.md#131-系统链路全景) integrated pipeline) |
| Optimization target | Conversions/GMV within the paid traffic | Overall GMV across the full site (paid + organic) |
| Measurement scope | GMV from ads (Direct attribution) | Full-site overall GMV increment (Broad attribution, paid + organic) |


Full-site promotion supports two bidding modes (mentioned in [§1.4.4](04.strategy-mechanisms.md#144-nobid 预算出价)):

- **GMVMAX-NoBid**: only set a daily budget; platform has full authority over bidding
- **GMVMAX-TargetROAS**: set a daily budget + target ROAS; platform optimizes under ROI constraints

Underlying mechanisms still use the bidding mechanisms from [§1.4.3](04.strategy-mechanisms.md#143-基础出价与智能出价) to [§1.4.5](04.strategy-mechanisms.md#145-双出价) (λ repricing, eCPM auction); the difference is that the optimization target expands from "maximize performance within paid traffic" to "maximize performance across full-site traffic."

> The integrated pipeline architecture diagram is in [§1.3.1](03.system-pipeline-modules.md#131-系统链路全景); the independent pipeline vs. integrated pipeline comparison is in [§1.4.6.2](04.strategy-mechanisms.md#1462-独立链路-vs-融合链路).

#### 1.5.2.2 为什么全站推广能驱动广告收入增长 / Why Full-Site Promotion Drives Advertising Revenue Growth

<!-- last_translated: 2026-06-05T09:27:44.779201+00:00 -->

Full-site promotion has become a growth engine for major e-commerce platforms because it achieves a paradigm upgrade in all four core modules of the advertising system — **traffic targeting, performance estimation, selling mechanism, and bidding strategy** — with changes in each module directly contributing to revenue growth.

**Traffic Targeting: From Rules to Models, Expanded Reach**


| Sub-module | Traditional Mode | Full-Site Promotion Mode |
| ----- | ------------------ | ----------------- |
| Recall targeting | Rule-based targeting (advertisers manually select keywords/audiences) | Model-based targeting (platform automatically matches users with products) |
| Recall/Pre-ranking | Ads and organic content each have independent recall pipelines | Ads and organic (native) supply pipelines are unified |


Revenue growth drivers:

- **Recall targeting**: Model-based targeting expands ad reach, reducing underspending caused by overly narrow targeting
- **Recall/Pre-ranking**: Unified supply pool enables ads to compete for more traffic

**Performance Estimation: Unified Models, Improved Accuracy**


| Sub-module | Traditional Mode | Full-Site Promotion Mode |
| ---- | -------------------- | ------------ |
| Ranking model | Ads and organic have independent samples, features, and optimization objectives | Samples/features/objectives are unified and shared |


Revenue growth drivers:

- **Ranking model**: Larger training data volume, richer signals, more accurate estimation → more precise eCPM valuation → advertisers willing to pay more for more accurate performance

**Selling Mechanism: From Fixed Slots to Unified Blending, Traffic Value Fully Released**

In traditional mode, ads are shown in fixed slots. Full-site promotion upgrades this to **unified ranking blending** (see [§1.4.6.2](04.strategy-mechanisms.md#1462-独立链路-vs-融合链路) blending formula `RankScore = α × eCPM + β × OrgScore`), producing two direct revenue growth effects:

- **Greater leverage of ads over traffic**: Ads are no longer confined to fixed slots; they can compete with organic content for display at any position across the full site. The paid traffic pool expands from "ad slots" to "full-site traffic," significantly increasing the reachable traffic for individual advertisers.
- **More rational billing mechanism, preventing undervalued traffic**: Full-site promotion uses full-domain billing, with the organic item's OrgScore serving as a floor price (compare with the GSP deduction formula in [§1.4.7.3](04.strategy-mechanisms.md#1473-gsp-扣费公式推导)):

```
actual_deduction = (next_RankScore - β × OrgScore) / (α × pCTR) + δ
```

Organic content participates in the auction (with OrgScore as a floor), making traffic competition more robust and billing prices closer to the advertiser's true value, avoiding the issue in traditional mode where low-competition slots are sold at undervalued prices.

**Bidding Strategy: Aligning Advertiser and Platform Value Objectives**


| Sub-module | Traditional Mode | Full-Site Promotion Mode |
| ---- | --------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| Bid expression | Manual CPC/CPA (manual bidding) | oCPX auto-bidding |
| Attribution scope | Direct attribution (counts only conversions directly from ads) | Broad attribution (counts conversions from paid + organic traffic) |
| Bid control | PID controller ([§1.4.3.3](04.strategy-mechanisms.md#1433-λ-调价系数)) | MPC (Model Predictive Control) |
| Budget allocation | Limited-scenario delivery (search only / recommendation only) | Full-domain delivery + intelligent control |


Revenue growth drivers:

- **Bid expression**: The "angle" between eCPM and OrgScore is smaller (see below); advertiser and platform value objectives are aligned
- **Attribution scope**: Broad attribution better matches advertisers' business goals; advertisers are willing to invest more budget
- **Bid control**: MPC is more precise; higher budget utilization rate; fewer campaign pauses due to cost overruns
- **Budget allocation**: Budget is no longer confined to a single scenario; the platform can seek optimal traffic across the full domain, improving overall consumption

**Understanding the Smaller "Angle"**

Revisiting the blending formula from [§1.4.6.2](04.strategy-mechanisms.md#1462-独立链路-vs-融合链路): `RankScore = α × eCPM + β × OrgScore`, where eCPM represents commercial value and OrgScore represents the platform's organic value (user experience, product quality, ecosystem health). Think of eCPM and OrgScore as **two directions**: when they are aligned (small "angle"), the ranking result both improves commercial revenue and protects user experience; when they conflict (large "angle"), the platform is forced into difficult trade-offs between revenue and experience.

Under traditional CPC manual bidding:

```
eCPM = CPC × pCTR
```

CPC is manually set by the advertiser and reflects the advertiser's "willingness to bid" rather than the "true value of the product to users." An advertiser with mediocre quality but aggressive bidding can buy good positions with high CPC; in this case eCPM is high but OrgScore is low — the two directions conflict, "angle" is large. The platform is forced to choose between "high revenue but poor experience" and "good experience but lower revenue"; ad load is constrained and there is a ceiling on traffic monetization.

Under full-site promotion's oCPX bidding:

```
eCPM = pGMV × λ / Target_ROAS
```

where `pGMV = pCTR × pCVR × pAOV` ([§1.4.3.4](04.strategy-mechanisms.md#1434-两种典型的成本出价表达模式)). The core driver of eCPM becomes **pGMV (predicted GMV)** — the model predicts how much transaction value this product can generate for this user. OrgScore also measures the value of the product to users (ratings, relevance, service quality, etc.). Products with high pGMV tend to be products that users like and are willing to buy, so OrgScore tends to be high as well — the two directions converge, and the "angle" shrinks.

Direct revenue growth effects from the smaller "angle":

- **Ad load can be safely increased**: When eCPM and OrgScore are aligned, displaying more ads does not significantly harm the user experience; the platform can increase ad load (more traffic is monetized)
- **Higher ranking efficiency**: No longer forced into difficult trade-offs between revenue and experience via α/β weights; the two objectives are naturally compatible, and RPM per impression improves
- **Positive cycle formed**: High ad quality → good user experience → user activity does not decline → traffic pool does not shrink → monetizable traffic continuously grows

**Synergistic Effects of the Four Modules**

The upgrades to the four modules do not work in isolation — they form a positive cycle:

- **More accurate performance estimation** → advertisers get better ROI → willing to increase budget
- **More efficient selling mechanism** → each unit of traffic generates higher revenue → platform RPM improves
- **Smarter bidding strategy** → higher budget utilization, more efficient advertiser campaigns → overall advertiser consumption increases

This explains why platforms like Pinduoduo, Alimama, Juhao Engine, and Shopee all saw significant advertising revenue growth after launching full-site promotion — full-site promotion is not simply "selling a few more ad slots," but a **full-pipeline upgrade** from performance estimation to selling to bidding.

From the advertiser's perspective, the core value of full-site promotion is:

- **Extremely simple to operate**: just set a budget (+ optional ROI target); no need to set bids, targeting, or keywords
- **Paid ads leverage organic traffic**: ad investment not only brings paid impressions but also indirectly increases organic traffic by boosting product popularity/weight
- **Overall ROI may be better than pure paid ads**: evaluated on full-site GMV rather than just ad GMV; broader Broad attribution scope
- **Suitable for merchants without campaign experience**: especially useful for small and medium merchants, new stores, new product launches, etc.

### 1.5.3 QCPX：智能优惠券出价 / QCPX: Smart Voucher Bidding

#### 1.5.3.1 什么是 QCPX / What Is QCPX

<!-- last_translated: 2026-06-05T09:27:36.407955+00:00 -->

QCPX = Voucher (Q) + oCPX. "Q" comes from the first letter of the Chinese word "券" (voucher), indicating that smart vouchers are stacked on top of oCPX smart bidding.

QCPX is an advertising product form in which the advertising platform, building on oCPX smart bidding, issues smart vouchers to users **at the platform's expense**, improving conversion rates by lowering users' payment threshold. Unlike oCPX, which only adjusts "bids," QCPX adds a "subsidy" dimension on top of "bidding."

Core differences between QCPX and oCPX:

| Dimension | oCPX ([§1.4.3.2](04.strategy-mechanisms.md#1432-智能出价-smart-bidding-ocpx)) | QCPX |
| ------- | ----------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| Optimization lever | Adjust bid | Adjust bid + issue vouchers (bid + voucher) |
| Who pays | Advertisers pay all ad fees | Advertisers pay ad fees + platform funds voucher costs |
| Conversion improvement mechanism | Find high-converting users through pCTR/pCVR models | On top of model-based user selection, further lift CVR by lowering payment threshold via vouchers |
| Impact on eCPM | `eCPM = Target_CPA × pCVR × pCTR × λ` | pCVR improves due to voucher issuance → eCPM increases → ad competitiveness strengthens |
| Core models | pCTR, pCVR, λ repricing | On top of oCPX models, add uplift model (predicts incremental conversions from voucher issuance) |

> The definition of oCPX and the eCPM formula are in [§1.4.3.2](04.strategy-mechanisms.md#1432-智能出价-smart-bidding-ocpx); the λ repricing coefficient is in [§1.4.3.3](04.strategy-mechanisms.md#1433-λ-调价系数); QCPX implementation details are in [§2.5](../02.ads-strategy/05.ads-smart-voucher.md#25-广告智能优惠券) Ads Smart Voucher.

#### 1.5.3.2 为什么平台愿意自己出钱发券 / Why the Platform Is Willing to Fund Vouchers Itself

<!-- last_translated: 2026-06-05T09:27:39.076998+00:00 -->

Core logic: from the platform's perspective, the cost is `voucher_cost` (voucher cost), and the revenue from issuing vouchers is `rev_uplift` (incremental ad revenue from voucher issuance). Net benefit = `rev_uplift - voucher_cost`. As long as the net benefit > 0, issuing vouchers is a **positive-ROI platform investment**.

**Platform perspective (why it's profitable):**

- **Voucher issuance boosts CVR → eCPM increases → ad consumption grows**: Vouchers lower users' payment threshold → pCVR increases → the pCVR component in the eCPM formula grows → ads become more competitive in blending → win more impressions → advertiser budget is consumed faster → platform ad revenue increases (i.e., `rev_uplift`)
- **Voucher issuance expands the advertiser's investable range**: Some advertisers with lower ROI achieve insufficient performance under pure oCPX and pause campaigns; after subsidy via vouchers, performance reaches targets and campaigns are reactivated → both advertiser count and consumption grow simultaneously for the platform
- **Platform can precisely control voucher ROI**: Through the uplift model, estimate the incremental conversion contribution of each voucher; only issue vouchers when `rev_uplift > voucher_cost` (positive net benefit), ensuring overall positive ROI for the platform's voucher issuance behavior

**Advertiser perspective (why they benefit):**

- **No additional cost**: Vouchers are funded by the platform; advertisers do not need to pay extra
- **Higher conversion rate**: Users' purchase intention strengthens after seeing vouchers; advertisers' ad performance (GMV/ROAS) improves
- **Lower cold start barrier**: For new product/new store advertisers during the phase of accumulating conversion data, subsidy from vouchers helps scale quickly

#### 1.5.3.3 QCPX 的技术框架 / QCPX Technical Framework

<!-- last_translated: 2026-06-05T09:27:36.941142+00:00 -->

QCPX contains three core modules (overview introduction; implementation details in [§2.5](../02.ads-strategy/05.ads-smart-voucher.md#25-广告智能优惠券)):

**1. Uplift Model: Deciding "Who Gets a Voucher and How Much"**

- Core question: estimate the **incremental conversion rate** for a given user after issuing a voucher (rather than the absolute conversion rate)
- Difference from oCPX's pCVR model: pCVR estimates "whether a user will convert"; the uplift model estimates "whether a voucher can convert a user who otherwise wouldn't have"
- Vouchers are only issued to users with sufficiently high uplift values, avoiding wasting subsidies on users who "would have purchased anyway"

**2. Voucher Issuance Control: Deciding "How Much Budget to Spend on Vouchers"**

- Long-window control: estimate optimal voucher budget allocation based on historical "budget → ROI" relationship
- Short-window control: monitor voucher consumption pacing in real time, balancing issuance speed with performance

**3. Separation of Ranking and Billing: Clarifying Platform Value vs. Advertiser Value**

- Ranking stage: rank by comprehensive revenue to the platform; ranking eCPM = `eCPM_0 + rev_uplift - voucher_cost`, where `eCPM_0` is the original eCPM, `rev_uplift` is the revenue increment from voucher issuance, and `voucher_cost` is the voucher cost
- Billing stage: billing with the advertiser is always settled based on ADVV (Ads Value to Advertiser), i.e., billing eCPM = `eCPM_0 + rev_uplift`; voucher cost is not deducted — the voucher is the platform's investment and should not be passed on to the advertiser
