## 1.4 广告系统策略机制 / Advertising System Strategy Mechanisms

### 1.4.1 规则与战术：机制 vs. 策略 / Rules and Tactics: Mechanisms vs. Strategies

<!-- last_translated: 2026-06-05T10:23:52.155896+00:00 -->

Core analogy: **Mechanism = Rules of the Game | Strategy = Tactics of the Game**


| Attribute | Mechanism | Strategy |
| ---- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Definition | The underlying framework and fundamental rules of the system, determining "what can be done" and "how to settle accounts." It is the system's "laws of physics." | Dynamic algorithms and optimization logic designed within the mechanism framework to achieve specific goals. It is the system's "intelligent decision-making." |
| Purpose | Ensure the market operates fairly, transparently, and stably; lay the foundation for long-term system health; guide participant behavior. | Maximize specific goals (e.g., platform revenue, advertiser ROI, user experience) within rule constraints. |
| Design Principles | **Equal treatment for all**: stability, fairness, predictability, incentive compatibility (e.g., GSP encourages truthful bidding). | **Personalization**: accuracy (estimation), real-time responsiveness (decision-making), adaptability (tuning), multi-objective balancing. |
| Ad Examples | Billing formulas: GSP (Generalized Second-Price), GFP (Generalized First-Price); billing methods: CPM, CPC, oCPX; ranking factors: which variables (e.g., bid, pCTR, quality score) and parameter weights are included in the ranking formula; admission rules: ad review standards | Bid expression: how advertisers express their target bid; smart bidding: dynamic repricing logic for oCPX; budget control: bidding algorithms for budget smoothing (Pacing); budget allocation: budget allocation across time periods and traffic scenarios |
| Impact of Changes | Far-reaching, low frequency of modification — like amending a constitution. | Continuously iterated with frequent A/B testing — like adjusting tactics. |

### 1.4.2 广告出价和售卖的核心概念 / Core Concepts of Ad Bidding and Selling

#### 1.4.2.1 常见计费模型 / Common Billing Models

<!-- last_translated: 2026-06-05T10:23:30.545319+00:00 -->

| Billing Model | Full Name       | When Advertiser Pays    | Who Bears the Risk                                                                          |
| ------------- | --------------- | ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **CPM**       | Cost Per Mille  | Every 1,000 impressions | Advertiser bears the efficiency risk                                                        |
| **CPC**       | Cost Per Click  | Each click              | Platform bears the click efficiency risk (fewer charges when CTR is low)                    |
| **CPA**       | Cost Per Action | Each conversion         | Platform bears both click and conversion efficiency risks (fewer charges when CTCVR is low) |


Among the four key "points," the **Ranking Point** is almost always impression (ranked by eCPM) — the advertising system's fundamental design rarely changes the ranking point. The **Evaluation Point** is the advertiser's objective business goal (e.g., ROAS, GMV), determined by the advertiser's own business model and not subject to change by the platform's system design. Therefore, the **Billing Point** and **Bid Point** are the core design variables for advertising system mechanism strategy, and the differences between various ad products described below (manual bidding vs. smart bidding) are primarily reflected in choices made for these two.

#### 1.4.2.2 为什么竞价点始终是 impression / Why the Ranking Point Is Always Impression

<!-- last_translated: 2026-06-05T10:23:33.543936+00:00 -->

- **The smallest decision unit for traffic allocation**: Each impression opportunity is the smallest granularity at which the platform allocates traffic. The platform needs to decide which ad to show at each impression, so ranking must occur at the impression level
- **A fair baseline for unified comparison**: Ads with different bid modes (CPC, CPA, CPM) compete for the same slot and must be converted to a unified dimension for fair comparison. Impression is an event that every ad necessarily goes through; eCPM (value per thousand impressions) is the only unified metric that makes ads with different bid modes comparable
- **The direct lever for maximizing platform revenue**: Platform ad revenue = Σ monetization value per impression; ranking by eCPM is directly equivalent to maximizing revenue from each impression opportunity, making it the optimal ranking strategy for platform revenue
- **No dependence on uncertain future events**: An impression is a deterministic event; clicks/conversions are probabilistic events. An impression is confirmed the moment the ad is served, without depending on subsequent user actions like clicking or converting, so ranking decisions can be made in real time with no delay or uncertainty
- Summary: An impression is the only event in the advertising system that simultaneously satisfies: being a platform-controllable atomic resource, being a deterministic event, occurring at the moment of decision, and serving as a unified comparison baseline. This is why the ranking point almost never changes — it is not a design choice but is determined by the nature of ad transactions.

#### 1.4.2.3 出价点与考核点的差距 / The Gap Between Bid Point and Evaluation Point

<!-- last_translated: 2026-06-05T10:23:58.053305+00:00 -->

The bid point is the optimization objective that advertisers communicate to the platform; the evaluation point is the final standard by which advertisers measure their own business performance. **Ideally the two should be unified** — advertisers should bid on what they evaluate, so the platform directly optimizes the goal that advertisers ultimately care about.

In practice, however, a gap may still exist between the two, primarily constrained by the following technical and business factors:

- **Predictability**: The bid point must be a goal that the platform's model can estimate in real time. Some evaluation metrics (e.g., LTV, long-term repeat GMV) depend on long-cycle data, and current model accuracy is insufficient to support real-time bidding decisions
- **Attributability**: The conversion event corresponding to the bid point needs to be clearly attributable to a single ad interaction. Some evaluation metrics involve cross-cycle, cross-channel comprehensive effects (e.g., brand awareness lift), where attribution boundaries are blurry
- **Data sparsity**: The deeper the conversion event (e.g., purchase, repeat purchase), the sparser the samples, the greater the difficulty of model training, and the higher the estimation variance

> As model capabilities improve and data infrastructure matures, the gap between the bid point and evaluation point will continue to narrow. When the platform can accurately predict the advertiser's final evaluation goal, the two can be truly unified.

To strengthen alignment between the platform's optimization target (bid point) and the advertiser's ultimate goal (evaluation point), the evolution of ad products has proceeded primarily in three parallel directions:

- **Bid point progressively approaching evaluation point**: CPC (bid point = click) → oCPA (bid point = conversion) → GMV MAX (bid point ≈ GMV), allowing the platform to directly optimize for the advertiser's final business goal.
- **Introducing Enhance Mode (enhance point) [transitional approach]**: When the bid point has not yet fully aligned with the evaluation point, the platform stacks a deeper optimization signal as an "enhance point" on top of the existing bid mode. For example, an advertiser bids by CPC, but the platform additionally introduces pCVR signals during competitive ranking, directing traffic toward higher-converting ads without changing the bid mode. This bidding mode is called eCPC (Enhanced CPC). The core value of Enhance Mode is: it does not change the advertiser's bidding habits and cost structure, while making the platform's traffic allocation more aligned with the advertiser's deeper goals. It is a transitional approach in the evolution from the current bid point toward the final evaluation point.
- **Dual Bidding**: Advertisers simultaneously set bid targets for both shallow and deep conversions (e.g., bidding on both clicks and completed orders simultaneously), and the platform jointly optimizes both targets in a single auction. Unlike the first direction, dual bidding does not replace the shallow bid point with a deep one; instead, it allows advertisers to explicitly express multi-level optimization intent. Unlike Enhance Mode, the deep target is not an implicit signal added by the platform, but a constraint actively set by the advertiser. Dual bidding is suitable for scenarios where advertisers care about both shallow efficiency (e.g., click cost) and deep performance (e.g., conversion cost), providing an explicit expression mechanism that balances multiple objectives before the bid point is fully unified with the evaluation point.

### 1.4.3 基础出价与智能出价 / Manual Bidding and Smart Bidding

#### 1.4.3.1 基础出价 / Manual Bidding

<!-- last_translated: 2026-06-05T10:23:29.780416+00:00 -->

The core characteristic of manual bidding is that the **bid point and billing point are unified** — advertisers directly set the bid amount by the billing event, and the platform charges at that amount.

| Bid Mode | Bid Point | Billing Point | Advertiser Sets | eCPM Conversion (for auction ranking) | Actual Charge |
| ------ | --- | --- | --------------- | ----------------- | ------------------------------ |
| CPM Bidding | Impression | Impression | CPM price (bid per 1,000 impressions) | eCPM = CPM price | Charged per impression, Cost = CPM × Impressions / 1000 |
| CPC Bidding | Click | Click | CPC price (bid per click) | eCPM = CPC × pCTR | Charged per click, Cost = CPC × Clicks |

Characteristics of manual bidding:

- **Transparent and controllable**: Advertisers know exactly the maximum cost per impression/click, and budget consumption is fully predictable
- **Optimization responsibility lies with the advertiser**: The platform only handles eCPM ranking and billing, without proactively optimizing conversion performance. Advertisers need to independently determine reasonable bids, select targeting audiences, and optimize creatives
- **Applicable scenarios**: Brand exposure (CPM), advertisers with mature campaign experience (CPC), scenarios requiring precise control of per-action costs

> Under manual bidding, the platform's eCPM ranking formula is directly determined by the advertiser's bid: CPM bidding needs no conversion; CPC bidding is converted via `eCPM = CPC × pCTR`. The only model estimation introduced by the platform is pCTR (for CPC→eCPM conversion), with limited optimization depth.

#### 1.4.3.2 智能出价（Smart Bidding / oCPX） / Smart Bidding (oCPX)

<!-- last_translated: 2026-06-05T10:23:49.857682+00:00 -->

The core characteristic of smart bidding is the **separation of bid point and billing point** — the bid point shifts downstream to deeper conversion events (e.g., CPA, GMV), while the billing point remains at impression or click. Advertisers only need to set a business-level target cost (e.g., Target CPA), and the platform automatically calculates the bid for each auction through model estimation.

| Bid Mode | Bid Point | Billing Point | Advertiser Sets | eCPM Conversion (for auction ranking) | Actual Charge |
| ---- | --- | --- | ---------- | ----------------------------------- | ----- |
| oCPC | Conversion | Click | Target CPA | eCPM = Target_CPA × pCVR × pCTR × λ | Charged per click |
| oCPM | Conversion | Impression | Target CPA | eCPM = Target_CPA × pCVR × pCTR × λ | Charged per impression |

Core differences between smart bidding and manual bidding:

| Comparison Dimension | Manual Bidding | Smart Bidding (oCPX) |
| ------- | ------------------- | ----------------------- |
| Bid Point vs. Billing Point | Unified (bid = billing) | Separated (bid point is deeper; billing point unchanged) |
| Advertiser Sets | Specific price per impression/click | Target business cost (Target CPA) |
| Model Dependency | Only pCTR (for CPC→eCPM conversion) | pCTR + pCVR + λ repricing coefficient |
| Optimization Responsibility | Advertisers optimize bids and conversions themselves | Platform bears the responsibility for conversion optimization |
| Risk Allocation | Conversion risk borne by advertisers | Conversion risk partially transferred to the platform |
| Applicable Scenarios | Brand exposure, mature campaign teams | Performance advertisers pursuing conversions (mainstream in Shopee) |

> The essence of smart bidding is that **the platform exchanges model estimation capability for a deeper optimization space**: manual bidding only introduces pCTR; smart bidding further introduces pCVR and the λ repricing coefficient, allowing the platform to perform global optimization across the entire impression → click → conversion funnel. Advertisers shift from "manually adjusting bids" to "setting goals and letting the platform auto-optimize," lowering the barrier to campaign management while giving the platform greater freedom in traffic allocation.

#### 1.4.3.3 λ 调价系数 / The λ Repricing Coefficient

<!-- last_translated: 2026-06-05T10:23:50.040168+00:00 -->

In the oCPX/tROAS formulas above, `Target_CPA × pCVR × pCTR` is the "theoretical bid" based on the advertiser's goal and model estimation. However, model estimation is imperfect and subject to real-world constraints such as conversion randomness. λ is the **feedback control knob** that bridges the gap between theory and reality. Its core control objective is **cost achievement** — ensuring that actual CPA/ROAS approaches the target value set by the advertiser. Hence oCPX/tROAS is also referred to as the "cost bidding" mode (contrasted with the budget bidding mode described below):

| Objective | Role of λ | Mechanism |
| ----------------------- | ---------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| **Budget Control (Budget Pacing)** | Controls the pace of daily budget consumption | Too fast → λ < 1 to reduce bids and slow down; insufficient → λ > 1 to increase bids and accelerate |
| **Cost Control** | Ensures actual CPA meets the advertiser's target | Actual CPA > Target → λ decreases; Actual CPA < Target → λ increases |
| **Cold Start Exploration** | Gives new ads exposure opportunities when historical data is lacking | λ is set higher during the initial stage and gradually normalized as data accumulates |

λ is dynamically adjusted by the offline bidding system (e.g., ultrav-core) through feedback control mechanisms such as PID, driving the actual campaign performance (spend pace, CPA achievement) to continuously approach the goals set by the advertiser.

#### 1.4.3.4 两种典型的成本出价表达模式 / Two Typical Cost Bidding Expression Modes

<!-- last_translated: 2026-06-05T10:23:46.004168+00:00 -->

| Bid Mode | Advertiser Sets | Platform Optimization Goal | eCPM Formula | Description |
| --------------------- | ----------------- | ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------- | --------------------------- |
| **oCPX (Target Cost Bidding)** | Target CPA + daily budget | Maximize conversions within the Target CPA constraint | eCPM = Target_CPA × pCVR × pCTR × λ | The most basic smart bidding mode; advertisers explicitly set a target cost per conversion |
| **tROAS (Target ROAS Bidding)** | Target ROAS + daily budget | Maximize GMV within the Target ROAS constraint | eCPM = pAOV × pCVR × pCTR × λ / Target_ROAS | The bid point shifts from conversion count to GMV; suitable for advertisers focused on transaction returns |

#### 1.4.3.5 为什么演进方向是”计费点前移 vs 出价点后移” / Why the Evolution Direction Is "Billing Point Shifts Forward vs. Bid Point Shifts Backward"

<!-- last_translated: 2026-06-05T10:23:28.231446+00:00 -->

In the advertising funnel, events occur in order: served → impression → click → order → payment → transaction. From manual bidding to smart bidding, the billing point and bid point evolve in opposite directions:

```
Billing point shift left ◀──────────────────────────────────▶ Bid point shift right

Served ← Impression(imp) ← Click(click) ← Order(order)    Order → Payment → Transaction → LTV
```

**(1) Why should the billing point shift as far forward (order → click → imp → served) as possible?**

Core consideration: **Platform revenue certainty and risk control**

- **High certainty**: Earlier events (impression/served) are more certain to occur, making platform revenue more stable. With impression-based billing, revenue is generated as soon as an ad is shown; with conversion-based billing, platform revenue depends on uncontrollable subsequent user behavior
- **Stronger anti-fraud capability**: Earlier events (impression/click) occur on the platform side, and the platform can directly verify their authenticity; later events (orders/payments) involve off-site or delayed actions, making fraud detection more difficult
- **Timely cash flow**: The earlier the billing point, the shorter the platform's revenue collection cycle, the healthier the cash flow
- **Lower system complexity**: Earlier events are available in real time, with no need to wait for long attribution windows and conversion callbacks

**(2) Why should the bid point shift as far backward (imp → click → order → payment → transaction) as possible?**

Core consideration: **Advertiser ROI controllability and platform optimization space**

- **Advertiser perspective**: The further the bid point shifts, the closer the advertiser's cost is to real business value. Bidding at CPC means advertisers bear the risk of "clicks that don't convert"; bidding at CPA/GMV transfers this risk to the platform, making the advertiser's ROI more controllable
- **Platform optimization space**: The further the bid point shifts, the more funnel stages the platform can optimize. Bidding at CPC means the platform only optimizes impression → click; bidding at CPA means the platform can simultaneously optimize impression → click → conversion, yielding higher overall efficiency
- **Lower barrier for advertisers**: A shifted-back bid point means advertisers don't need to understand intermediate metrics like CTR/CVR — they only need to focus on final business costs, lowering the professional barrier to campaign management

> **In essence, this is a risk allocation game between the platform and advertisers**: moving the billing point forward locks in platform revenue and reduces risk; moving the bid point backward locks in advertiser ROI and reduces their risk. The gap between the two represents the optimization responsibility borne by the platform — the platform bridges this gap through model estimation of pCTR/pCVR. The more accurate the model, the less risk the platform bears, while the advertiser experience improves simultaneously.

### 1.4.4 Nobid/预算出价 / NoBid / Budget Bidding

#### 1.4.4.1 成本出价 VS 预算出价 / Cost Bidding vs. Budget Bidding

<!-- last_translated: 2026-06-05T10:24:11.696097+00:00 -->

Smart bidding can be divided into cost-based bidding and budget-based bidding based on the control objective of the λ repricing coefficient:

- **Cost-based Bidding** means the advertiser sets a business-level **target cost** (e.g., Target CPA or Target ROAS), and the platform automatically calculates the eCPM for each auction using model estimation (pCTR, pCVR, etc.) and the repricing coefficient λ.
  - The core control objective of λ is **cost achievement** — ensuring that actual CPA/ROAS approaches the target value set by the advertiser, while also considering budget smoothing.
  - However, these two objectives can conflict: the cost constraint and budget constraint are two independent constraints sharing the same control variable λ. When the advertiser's target cost is tight relative to market prices (i.e., the total volume of traffic satisfying the cost constraint is insufficient to exhaust the budget), honoring the cost means the budget can't be spent; spending the budget means exceeding the cost. Conversely, when the advertiser's target cost is loose (i.e., the consumption volume of traffic satisfying the cost constraint exceeds the set daily budget), honoring the cost means the budget hits its ceiling early; smoothly spending throughout the campaign period means missing cost targets. In practice, most platforms adopt a **cost-first** strategy — preferring to underspend or exhaust the budget early rather than exceeding the cost target — because advertisers who set a Target CPA have higher certainty requirements for per-conversion cost than for budget consumption. In engineering, a common approach is to decompose λ into two independent components `λ = λ_cost × λ_pacing`, which track cost deviation and budget consumption progress separately, with a priority weight determining final behavior when the two conflict (λ_cost typically has higher priority).
- **Budget-based Bidding (NoBid)** means the advertiser **only sets a daily budget** without any target cost, leaving all bidding decisions to the platform. The platform autonomously determines the eCPM for each auction within the budget constraint, with the goal of maximizing conversions or GMV.
  - The core control objective of λ is **budget consumption** — ensuring the budget is smoothly spent within the campaign period while maximizing overall performance.
  - These two objectives are fundamentally aligned: an unspent budget means foregone conversions that could have been obtained, so "spending the budget" is a necessary condition for maximizing conversions. Given a fixed total budget, maximizing conversions is equivalent to spending each unit of money on the traffic with the highest marginal conversion value — if spending is concentrated in one period, competition increases and per-conversion cost rises; if spending is insufficient in a period, low-cost conversion opportunities are missed. Therefore, the optimal budget allocation strategy naturally requires smooth consumption aligned with the value distribution of traffic — precisely what λ achieves as the Lagrange multiplier for the budget constraint: λ dynamically adjusts bid levels so that marginal conversion costs tend toward equilibrium across time periods and traffic sources, simultaneously achieving "spend it all" and "spend it well."

| Comparison Dimension | Cost Bidding (oCPX / tROAS) | Budget Bidding (NoBid / MCB / BCB) |
| ------ | ---------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| Advertiser Sets | Target cost (Target CPA / ROAS) + daily budget | Daily budget only (no bidding required) |
| Core λ Objective | Cost achievement first, budget smoothing secondary | Budget consumption first, maximize performance |
| Platform Optimization Goal | Maximize conversions/GMV within the target cost constraint | Maximize conversions/GMV within the budget constraint |
| Advertiser Control | Medium — advertisers constrain the platform's bid range via target cost | Lowest — advertisers only control how much to spend, not how |
| Applicable Scenarios | Advertisers with clear requirements for per-conversion cost / ROI | Advertisers pursuing maximum performance or unconcerned about per-action cost; new advertisers with limited experience |
| Typical Products | oCPX (target CPA), tROAS (target ROAS) | MCB (Maximum Conversion Bidding), BCB (Budget Controlled Bidding) |

> From cost bidding to budget bidding, the advertiser's configuration becomes increasingly simple (from setting a target cost to only setting a budget), while the platform's optimization responsibility grows larger (from optimizing within cost constraints to making all bidding decisions). The core difference lies in λ's control objective: in cost bidding, λ prioritizes ensuring cost targets are met; in budget bidding, λ prioritizes ensuring the budget is smoothly spent.
>
> It is worth noting that both bidding modes have the same optimization goal of "maximize conversions/GMV" — they simply differ in constraints. For this reason, in some scenarios they are respectively called GMVMAX-TargetROAS and GMVMAX-NoBid, meaning both modes share the same evaluation point: conversion volume or GMV.

#### 1.4.4.2 NoBid 适用场景 / Applicable Scenarios for NoBid

<!-- last_translated: 2026-06-05T10:23:58.453509+00:00 -->

NoBid's core premise is "advertisers only set a budget, not a target cost," so applicable scenarios essentially depend on: **under what circumstances the advertiser's core constraint is the budget rather than the per-action cost** (i.e., "budget is fixed, cost is flexible").

| Applicable Scenario | Description | Typical Case |
| ------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| **Fixed budget, flexible cost** | The advertiser has a clear budget cap but has some tolerance for per-conversion cost; core demand is "spend this money well and fully" | Small and medium sellers, new brands with limited budget |
| **Short-duration campaigns** | Short campaign window (hours to a day); the λ in cost bidding doesn't have enough time to converge to a stable state; NoBid's budget pacing mechanism is a better fit | Live streaming promotions (2–4 hours), limited-time sales events (Double 11, Flash Sales), short drama/content premieres |
| **No cost baseline** | No historical data to judge a reasonable Target_CPA; forcing a number is likely to be inaccurate — better to let the platform explore under budget constraints | First-time campaigns for new advertisers, new products/markets/regions, first attempts at new ad formats (Video Ads / Live Ads) |
| **Pursuing maximum volume** | In a growth phase, core goal is to rapidly acquire users/orders at scale; sensitivity to per-action cost is lower than to total volume | New products driving sales to create bestsellers, gaining market share, rapidly accumulating model samples during cold start |
| **Agency budget execution** | Agencies manage campaigns on behalf of clients; budget is a hard constraint (must refund unspent budget), cost is a soft constraint; NoBid naturally fits the "given budget, maximize output" delegation structure | Agency/service provider managed campaigns |
| **Multi-campaign portfolio management** | Mature advertisers running multiple campaigns simultaneously, each using NoBid to maximize output internally, while managing overall ROI through budget allocation across campaigns — lifting cost control from single-campaign to portfolio level | Large advertisers with multiple campaigns running in parallel |

#### 1.4.4.3 Nobid 预算消耗规划 / NoBid Budget Consumption Planning

<!-- last_translated: 2026-06-05T10:24:20.403971+00:00 -->

The control objective of NoBid is to **smoothly spend the budget within the campaign period while maximizing conversions**. Implementation proceeds in two steps:

- Step 1, **formulate a consumption plan**: break the total budget into time periods, determining how much to spend in each period
- Step 2, **real-time tracking and execution**: track the consumption plan via λ (bid multiplier) — when actual consumption lags behind the plan, increase λ to raise bids and accelerate; when consumption is ahead, decrease λ to lower bids and decelerate

Step 2 is a universal control mechanism, so the core question becomes: **how to formulate the consumption plan?**

The table below lists strategies in ascending order of quality; the problem with each strategy motivates the improvement in the next:


| Strategy | Approach | Problem |
| ------------ | --------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| **① Spend early** | No planning; buy whenever traffic is available; stop when budget is exhausted | High-quality traffic in the latter half of the campaign window cannot be purchased at all, resulting in low overall conversion efficiency |
| **② Random fluctuation** | Consumption fluctuates with no clear allocation rules | Unpredictable performance; meaningful A/B testing or attribution becomes impossible |
| **③ Uniform distribution** | Allocate budget evenly over time (e.g., daily budget ÷ 24 hours) | Does not account for differences in traffic distribution — late-night traffic may be only 5% of the daily total, yet receives the same budget allocation as peak evening hours, causing abnormally high purchase ratios during low-traffic periods |
| **④ Distribution by traffic** | Allocate budget in proportion to historical traffic share for each period, so that the purchase ratio in each period aligns with the overall traffic distribution | Does not account for differences in competition intensity across periods — buying at the same ratio in all periods ignores the fact that periods with weaker competition offer better value |
| **⑤ Distribution by value ratio** | Comprehensively consider traffic volume and competition intensity; allocate more budget to periods with better value (weaker competition) | Theoretically optimal but hardest to implement in practice — value ratio is influenced by competitor bids, which are difficult to predict accurately in advance |


The mainstream NoBid strategy in the industry is based on Strategy ④ or ⑤, combined with the λ real-time tracking mechanism to form a "plan → track → adjust" closed loop.

#### 1.4.4.4 预算出价和成本出价优劣势对比 / Comparison of Advantages and Disadvantages: Budget Bidding vs. Cost Bidding

<!-- last_translated: 2026-06-05T10:23:53.269932+00:00 -->

| Comparison Dimension | Cost Bidding (oCPX / tROAS) | Budget Bidding (NoBid) |
| ------------ | -------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| **Cold Start Incentives** | Advantage: Platform subsidies + compensation; low cold start barrier for advertisers | Advantage: No compensation; advertisers bear their own costs, incentivizing quality creatives and reducing mindless volume stacking |
| | Disadvantage: Advertisers need not bear the cost of low-quality creatives, which can lead to adverse selection (stacking volume to game subsidies) | Disadvantage: Advertisers without experience may see higher cold start costs after subsidies are removed |
| **eCPM Control** | Advantage: Target_CPA is a first-order multiplicative factor in the eCPM formula; changes immediately alter eCPM, with immediate and controllable bidding power changes | Disadvantage: Can only indirectly influence λ by adjusting budget, which then influences eCPM — a second-order indirect effect with uncertain magnitude |
| **Intent Expression** | Advantage: Directly expresses the demand of "CPA/ROAS must be met" | Advantage: Directly expresses the demand of "spend this budget well," without needing to guess an appropriate target cost |
| | Disadvantage: Advertisers with limited budgets need to trial-and-error a suitable Target_CPA to exhaust the budget, and the trial-and-error process itself is an efficiency loss | Disadvantage: Cannot directly constrain per-conversion cost; cost is uncontrolled |
| **Risk Management & Bidding Aggressiveness** | Advantage: Cost protection mechanism is clear; per-conversion cost fluctuation is controllable | Advantage: Without a cost constraint as a conservative suppressor, bidding is more aggressive, offering opportunities to acquire more traffic and conversions |
| | Disadvantage: To protect costs, the platform tends toward conservative suppression (preferring to underspend than to exceed costs), potentially missing high-quality traffic | Disadvantage: Pacing still faces delayed callback challenges; risk concerns shift from the "cost dimension" to the "budget dimension" — the problem is not eliminated |
| **Campaign Setup Barrier** | Advantage: Just set a target cost; no need to over-focus on budget allocation; platform automatically optimizes to ensure cost achievement | Advantage: Only need to set a budget; no need to guess an appropriate target cost; simpler campaign setup |
| | Disadvantage: Must set the most suitable Target_CPA based on own marketing goals and budget; incorrect settings can lead to underspending or overly low ROI | Disadvantage: Must judge creative quality independently, formulate budget allocation strategies, and control risk exposure; high demands on optimizer experience |

> The two modes are **complementary rather than substitutes**, serving different core needs: cost bidding guarantees certainty of per-conversion cost, while budget bidding guarantees maximum efficiency of budget utilization. In practice they can be used in combination — for example, using budget bidding during cold start to accumulate data, then switching to cost bidding for scale.

#### 1.4.4.5 NoBid 的增长困境 / NoBid's Growth Dilemma

<!-- last_translated: 2026-06-05T10:23:52.793036+00:00 -->

NoBid lacks an ROI anchor, leading to a polarized ROI distribution among advertisers, with both ends lacking the motivation to increase budget — forming a "bilateral deadlock":


| Comparison Dimension | NoBid | Target ROAS |
| ------------- | ------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| **Low-ROI Advertisers** | No cost constraint as a safety net; ROI below the business breakeven → pause or reduce budget, losing existing revenue | ROI anchored at the target value; cost is controllable; high retention rate |
| **High-ROI Advertisers** | ROI far exceeds business needs, but expectation is "increasing budget = λ rises = ROI drops" → unwilling to increase budget | After increasing budget, platform automatically adjusts λ to maintain ROI → low resistance to budget expansion |
| **Growth Flywheel** | Uncontrollable ROI → loss + deadlock → stagnant budget pool → negative feedback | Controllable ROI → budget expansion → more data → more accurate model → more stable ROI → positive flywheel |


> The root cause of NoBid's growth difficulty: low-ROI advertisers leave due to uncontrolled costs, while high-ROI advertisers are locked into their budgets due to the expectation that "increasing budget reduces ROI." Both ends are simultaneously blocked, making it difficult for the revenue pool to expand. Target ROAS breaks this deadlock through explicit ROI constraints — budget expansion shifts from a "ROI gamble" to a "certain investment."

#### 1.4.4.6 业界解决方案 / Industry Solutions

<!-- last_translated: 2026-06-05T10:24:33.910389+00:00 -->

To address the growth dilemmas above, major advertising platforms have proposed different solutions centered around "adding ROI constraints on top of NoBid":


| Solution | Representative Platforms | Core Idea | Problem Addressed |
| --------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------- | ------------------------ |
| **NoBid + optional ROI floor** | Google (Maximize Conversion Value + optional Target ROAS), Pinduoduo (full-site promotion with target ROAS option) | Stack an optional ROAS constraint on top of NoBid; without it, pure budget spending; with it, ROI is protected | Low-ROI advertiser churn — provides a safety net |
| **Phased evolution** | TikTok (Maximum Delivery → Cost Cap / Target ROAS) | Use NoBid during cold start to accumulate 50+ conversions, then switch to cost bidding once data is stable | New advertisers lack data baselines and cannot set a reasonable ROAS |
| **Cost protection / compensation** | TikTok Smart+ (Incentive Offer), Meta (Minimum ROAS) | Refund ad fees if cost targets are not met, or set a ROAS floor and only bid when the prediction meets the threshold | High uncertainty for new advertisers on their first campaign, low willingness to try |
| **ROI internalized as optimization objective** | Meta (Maximize ROAS, renamed in 2025) | Algorithm objective shifts from "maximize total conversion value" to "maximize return per dollar spent" | Fundamentally upgrades ROI efficiency from a constraint to an optimization target |


> Core pattern: all solutions converge — stacking ROI-dimensional constraints or incentives on top of NoBid's budget consumption logic. The only difference is the form of constraint: optional ROAS floor, phased switching, automatic budget increase, cost compensation, or directly internalizing ROI as the optimization objective.
>
> It is worth noting that the above solutions primarily address the problem of "low-ROI advertiser churn," but do not truly solve the dilemma of "high-ROI advertisers unwilling to increase budget." Under pure NoBid, "increasing budget = ROI drop" is a mathematical certainty — as budget increases → λ rises → marginal traffic quality decreases → ROI falls. Without an ROI constraint, the system's goal is to spend the budget rather than maintain ROI, so this problem is unsolvable within the NoBid framework. The essence of all solutions is to transform NoBid into "NoBid with ROI constraints," thereby circumventing rather than solving the dilemma. The only way to let high-ROI advertisers comfortably increase their budget is for the system to commit that "ROI will remain stable after budget increases" — which is precisely the definition of Target ROAS.

### 1.4.5 双出价 / Dual Bidding

#### 1.4.5.1 为什么需要双出价 / Why Dual Bidding Is Needed

<!-- last_translated: 2026-06-05T10:24:13.217492+00:00 -->

**From the advertiser's perspective: single bidding cannot express real needs**

Before dual bidding emerged, advertisers could only choose one of two options:

| Option | Approach | Problem | Advertiser Pain Point |
| ----------------- | ---------------- | --------------------------------------------- | ----------------------- |
| **Shallow bidding** (oCPA-activation) | Only constrains activation cost; system only optimizes activation volume | System tends to find low-quality users who "easily activate but don't pay"; deep conversion performance is poor | Sufficient traffic, but low deep conversion revenue |
| **Deep bidding** (oCPA-payment) | Only constrains payment cost; system directly optimizes payments | Deep conversion events are extremely sparse (payment rate may be <1%); model estimation is inaccurate, hard to scale, and shallow costs may skyrocket out of control | Deep performance is good but hard to scale, or activation cost is too high, leading to losses |

> **The core contradiction dual bidding resolves**: advertisers want both controllable shallow costs (controlling the loss floor) and deep performance to meet targets (ensuring ROI). Single bidding can only address one end; dual bidding allows advertisers to fully express their business constraints.

**From the platform's perspective: improving the health and revenue ceiling of the ad ecosystem**

- **Retain deep-conversion advertisers and leverage budget**: Deep-conversion advertisers (gaming, e-commerce, finance, etc.) have large budgets but high performance requirements. Shallow bidding leads to poor performance and budget cuts; deep bidding leads to difficulty scaling and an inability to spend the budget. Dual bidding allows these advertisers to both scale and achieve performance, encouraging continued and increased investment
- **Improve traffic allocation efficiency**: With single shallow bidding, the system cannot distinguish between "high-quality activations" and "low-quality activations." After introducing deep signals via dual bidding, the system can allocate traffic to ads that truly generate deep value, improving overall traffic monetization efficiency
- **Alleviate model sparsity**: Pure deep bidding faces extremely sparse samples. Under the dual bidding framework, the shallow objective provides sufficient samples for model learning, while the deep objective selectively picks traffic on top of this — balancing model learnability and optimization precision

#### 1.4.5.2 竞价机制 / Auction Mechanism

<!-- last_translated: 2026-06-05T10:24:59.497272+00:00 -->

**Basic eCPM Calculation**

Under dual bidding, each ad has two independent sets of eCPM:

```
eCPM_shallow = bid_shallow × pCTR × pCVR_shallow
eCPM_deep    = bid_deep × pCTR × pCVR_shallow × pDVR
```
```

where pDVR (Deep Conversion Rate) = the conditional probability of going from shallow to deep conversion. The two eCPM sets respectively represent the upper bound of expected value for that traffic under each constraint.

**Auction Scoring**

Auction scoring needs to address three issues of different nature: **model calibration** (ratio), **cost achievement control** (λ), and **signal fusion** (ranking), plus tail risk filtering. The three are logically independent and are implemented as a single scoring computation:

```
```
eCPM_shallow' = eCPM_shallow × ratio_shallow × λ_shallow
eCPM_deep'    = eCPM_deep × ratio_deep × λ_deep
eCPM_final    = f(eCPM_shallow', eCPM_deep') × I(pDVR ≥ threshold)
```
```

Both ratio and λ are applied to each side's eCPM (before fusion), but they solve two essentially different problems and must be strictly distinguished.

**(1) Model Calibration (ratio): Making Future Estimates More Accurate**

Objective: correct systematic bias in model estimation, so that future pCoC (predicted CVR over actual CVR) tends toward 1. ratio is a **forward calibration coefficient** — it only cares about "how accurate future estimates are," not about "whether too much or too little was spent in the past."

```
```
ratio_shallow = actual_CVR_shallow / predicted_CVR_shallow    // Shallow model calibration
ratio_deep    = actual_DVR / predicted_DVR                    // Deep model calibration
```
```

- Model overestimates (pCoC > 1) → ratio < 1 → suppresses eCPM on that side
- Model underestimates (pCoC < 1) → ratio > 1 → lifts eCPM on that side
- Updated at a slower frequency (hourly), computed smoothly based on cumulative statistical data, calibrated independently on each side

**(2) Cost Achievement Control (λ_shallow, λ_deep): Ensuring Daily Cost Targets Are Met**

Objective: ensure that actual shallow and deep costs do not exceed the advertiser's bid constraints on a daily basis, compensating for any over- or under-spending that has already occurred. λ is a **backward compensation coefficient** — it cares not only about "whether future bids are reasonable" but also must account for "historical baggage from the past."

```
```
Shallow PID: monitors cumulative deviation of actual_cost_shallow vs bid_shallow → outputs λ_shallow
Deep PID:    monitors cumulative deviation of actual_cost_deep vs bid_deep    → outputs λ_deep
```

- Updated at a faster frequency (minute-level), regulated via PID controller based on real-time cost feedback
- λ_shallow and λ_deep are each independent, tracking their own cost deviations separately

> **Both ratio and λ are applied before fusion**, so that the fusion function operates on accurate, calibrated and controlled values. When one side is "screened out" by the fusion function (e.g., the non-bottleneck side under min fusion), it is precisely because that side's constraint is not tight and needs no control.

**(3) Essential Difference Between ratio and λ**


| Dimension | ratio (Model Calibration) | λ (Cost Achievement Control) |
| -------- | ------------------- | ----------------- |
| Problem Addressed | Accuracy of each future estimate | Achievement of cumulative daily cost targets |
| Time Direction | **Forward**: making future estimates accurate | **Backward**: compensating for past deviations |
| Bears Historical Baggage | No — only calibrates the estimate itself | Yes — must compensate for over- or under-spending |
| Data Source | pCoC statistics (predicted vs. actual) | Cumulative deviation of actual cost vs. target cost |
| Update Frequency | Slow (hourly) | Fast (minute-level) |
| Convergence Target | pCoC → 1 | Daily cost → bid × conversion count |


Cases where neither can replace the other:

- **ratio corrected but λ still necessary**: In the morning, inaccurate ratio_shallow leads to 500 CNY in shallow over-spending; by noon, ratio_shallow is corrected (pCoC ≈ 1) and future estimates are now accurate, but the 500 CNY over-spend is a historical fact → λ_shallow < 1 is needed in the afternoon to compensate
- **λ adjusted but ratio still necessary**: Model systematically overestimates pCVR by 20%; λ_shallow can control shallow costs by suppressing bids, but the ranking is wrong — overestimated ads rank ahead and squeeze out ads that should have won → ratio_shallow must first calibrate estimates so ranking is correct

**(4) Tail Filtering: Removing Extreme Low-Quality Traffic**

When pDVR is extremely low (e.g., 0.001%), the estimation variance itself is huge, and deep conversion attribution delays (potentially days) mean cost control cannot respond in time. For such traffic, set to zero directly:

`pDVR < threshold → eCPM_final = 0 (not eligible for auction)` 

How to determine the threshold: **static** `threshold = bid_shallow / bid_deep` (the critical point where the two sides' eCPMs are equal); **dynamic**: dynamically adjusted based on real-time deep cost feedback.

#### 1.4.5.3 eCPM 融合策略 / eCPM Fusion Strategies

<!-- last_translated: 2026-06-05T10:24:23.088953+00:00 -->

Core question: how to fuse the two calibrated and controlled eCPMs into a single final auction score.

| Strategy | Formula | Advantages | Disadvantages |
| ------------------- | ------------------------------- | -------------------- | --------------------------- |
| **Take minimum** | `min(eCPM_shallow', eCPM_deep')` | Strict cost control; high dual-cost achievement rate | Too conservative; bidding power is dragged down by the weaker side; hard to scale |
| **Weighted fusion** | `α × eCPM_shallow' + (1-α) × eCPM_deep'` | Flexible; tunable between scaling and performance | α and λ_shallow/λ_deep form multi-layer feedback loops; prone to coupled oscillation |
| **Shallow × deep adjustment** (industry mainstream) | `eCPM_shallow' × g(pDVR)` | Scaling anchored by shallow side; performance regulated by deep side; clear separation of responsibilities | Requires careful parameter tuning; g function depends on real-time statistics |

> Typical g function implementation in Strategy 3: `g(pDVR) = (pDVR / avg(pDVR)) ^ β`, where β controls the adjustment intensity. Traffic with pDVR above the average is boosted; traffic below the average is suppressed. Deep cost control (ratio_deep, λ_deep) is implemented indirectly by influencing the parameters of g() or the threshold.

**Relationship Between Fusion Strategy and Constraint Conflict**

The core contradiction of dual bidding is the directional conflict between the shallow and deep cost constraints: ensuring deep performance requires selective filtering (reduced volume), while ensuring shallow scaling requires relaxing deep selection. Different fusion strategies handle this conflict differently:

- **min()** naturally avoids conflict (always takes the tighter side), but bidding power is always dragged down by the weaker side
- **Weighted fusion**: α and λ_shallow/λ_deep form multi-layer feedback loops that may cause coupled oscillation — deep overspend → α↓ → overall bid drops → shallow consumption insufficient → λ_shallow↑ → overall bid recovers → deep overspend again. Mitigation approaches include limiting adjustment magnitude, setting priority (deep takes priority), and using different time scales for α and λ
- **Shallow × g(pDVR)**: responsibilities are orthogonally decoupled — the shallow side (including ratio_shallow × λ_shallow) manages scaling and shallow cost; g(pDVR) only performs relative ranking across traffic, with no coupling issues. This is one of the reasons this strategy has become industry mainstream

#### 1.4.5.4 完整竞价流程 / Complete Auction Process

<!-- last_translated: 2026-06-05T10:24:34.884624+00:00 -->

Request arrives
  │
  ├─ 1. Recall candidate ad set
  │
  ├─ 2. Model prediction: pCTR, pCVR_shallow, pDVR
  │
  ├─ 3. Auction scoring (single computation)
  │     a. Model calibration + cost control:
  │        eCPM_shallow' = eCPM_shallow × ratio_shallow × λ_shallow
  │        eCPM_deep' = eCPM_deep × ratio_deep × λ_deep
  │     b. Tail filtering: pDVR < threshold → eCPM_final = 0
  │     c. Fusion ranking: eCPM_final = f(eCPM_shallow', eCPM_deep')
  │
  ├─ 4. Unified ranking, winner gets impression
  │
  └─ 5. Post-hoc attribution
        a. Update pCoC stats → adjust ratio_shallow/ratio_deep (model calibration)
        b. Update cumulative cost deviation per side → PID adjusts λ_shallow/λ_deep (cost achievement control)

#### 1.4.5.5 核心难点 / Core Challenges

<!-- last_translated: 2026-06-05T10:24:15.764498+00:00 -->

- **Deep signal delay**: Deep conversions (e.g., payment) may occur hours or even days after a click. The feedback received by both ratio_deep and λ_deep is lagged. The statistics window for ratio_deep must be long enough to smooth noise; the PID controller for λ_deep needs to account for delay compensation
- **Sample sparsity**: The positive sample count for the pDVR model is extremely small (payment rate may be <1%); insufficient statistics for ratio_deep make calibration itself unstable
- **Two-sided tension**: Excessive selectivity to protect the deep side → insufficient volume on the shallow side; relaxing selectivity to protect volume → deep cost exceeds targets. ratio ensures the ordering correctness of each side; λ ensures cost controllability of each side; the two cooperate independently on each side to mitigate the tension

#### 1.4.5.6 双出价 vs 单出价总结 / Dual Bidding vs. Single Bidding Summary

<!-- last_translated: 2026-06-05T10:24:24.563842+00:00 -->

| Dimension | Single Shallow Bidding | Single Deep Bidding | Dual Bidding |
| ---- | ----- | ----- | ----------- |
| Scaling Capability | Strong | Weak | Strong |
| Deep Performance | Uncontrolled | Good | Controllable |
| Shallow Cost | Controllable | Uncontrolled | Controllable |
| Model Learning | Sufficient samples | Sparse samples | Shallow anchoring + Deep selection |

> The essence of dual bidding is **finding a balance between scaling (exploration) and performance (exploitation)**, allowing advertisers to fully express their business constraints and enabling the platform to allocate traffic more efficiently.

### 1.4.6 广告混排 / Ad Blending

#### 1.4.6.1 什么是混排 / What Is Ad Blending

<!-- last_translated: 2026-06-05T10:24:19.065226+00:00 -->

**Ad Blending (Ad Blending / Ad Mixing)** refers to the process of merging auction-winning ads with organic results into a unified display list that is ultimately presented to users. It is the core step in the **"Bidding & Mixing"** phase of the ad pipeline described in [§1.3.1](03.system-pipeline-modules.md#131-系统链路全景).

Ad blending is essentially a **three-party game** problem:

| Party | Goal | Demand in Blending |
| --- | --------- | ---------------- |
| Platform | Maximize ad revenue | Display as many high-eCPM ads as possible |
| Advertiser | Maximize campaign ROI | Ads appear in positions with high conversion potential |
| User | Best browsing experience | Ads do not disrupt content consumption and are relevant |

> The goal of blending is not to "show as many ads as possible," but to find the **Pareto optimum among revenue, ROI, and experience**.

#### 1.4.6.2 独立链路 vs 融合链路 / Independent Pipeline vs. Integrated Pipeline

<!-- last_translated: 2026-06-05T10:24:26.492942+00:00 -->

Shopee's advertising system has two blending architectures, corresponding to the two pipelines in [§1.3.1](03.system-pipeline-modules.md#131-系统链路全景):


| Dimension | Independent Pipeline (Standard Mode) | Integrated Pipeline (GMV MAX) |
| --------- | --------------------- | --------------------------- |
| Recall & Ranking | Ads are independently recalled and ranked | Ads and organic content are recalled and ranked together (API0–API3) |
| Blending Method | Ads are inserted into organic results at the final display stage | Fusion is complete at the ranking stage; no additional insertion needed |
| RankScore | Used for internal ad ranking; insertion decisions are made at the blending stage | Ads and organic content use a unified ranking score |
| Advantages | Simple architecture; ads and organic content are decoupled and can iterate independently | Globally optimal ranking; more consistent user experience |
| Disadvantages | Insertion positions may not be globally optimal | Complex architecture; high coupling between ads and organic content |

#### 1.4.6.3 混排策略：固定坑位 vs 动态插入 / Blending Strategies: Fixed Slots vs. Dynamic Insertion

<!-- last_translated: 2026-06-05T10:24:26.711147+00:00 -->

Based on ad type (refer to the classification in [§1.2.3.1](02.online-advertising-fundamentals.md#1231-按投放机制分类)), blending strategies fall into two main categories:

| Dimension | Fixed Slots (Reserved Slot) | Dynamic Insertion |
| ------------ | ------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------ |
| Mechanism | Specific positions on the page are reserved for ads and do not compete in ranking | Ads compete with organic content for display positions based on RankScore |
| Role of RankScore | Only determines which ad is selected within the slot | Determines whether an ad is inserted and where |
| Ad Load Control | Fixed number of slots; load is naturally controlled | Requires additional density constraints and frequency capping rules |
| Applicable Ad Types | Display ads, brand ads ([§1.2.3.1](02.online-advertising-fundamentals.md#1231-按投放机制分类)) | Performance ads, search ads ([§1.2.3.1](02.online-advertising-fundamentals.md#1231-按投放机制分类)) |

#### 1.4.6.4 广告负载与体验约束 / Ad Load and Experience Constraints

<!-- last_translated: 2026-06-05T10:24:48.301871+00:00 -->

To protect the user experience, a series of **Experience Constraints** are applied during the blending stage:

| Constraint Type | Description | Example |
| ------------------------ | --------------------- | ---------------- |
| **Ad Load Cap** | The proportion of ads per page/screen must not exceed a threshold | Ad density on the search results page ≤ 20% |
| **Minimum Organic Gap (Min Gap)** | At least N organic results must appear between two consecutive ads | At least 2 organic items between adjacent ads |
| **Same-Shop Cap** | The maximum number of ads from the same shop in a single request | ≤ 2 ads from the same shop |
| **Session-Level Impression Cap** | Maximum number of impressions for the same ad in a single user session | Same ad shown no more than 3 times in a session |
| **Position Rule** | Certain ad types can only appear within specified position ranges | Brand ads only appear within the first 3 positions on the first screen |

These constraints are enforced as **Hard Constraints** during blending decisions — even if an ad's RankScore is very high, ads that violate experience constraints will not be displayed.

#### 1.4.6.5 排序公式与多目标优化 / Ranking Formula and Multi-Objective Optimization

<!-- last_translated: 2026-06-05T10:24:27.945822+00:00 -->

The core ranking basis for blending is the **RankScore formula** from [§1.3.2.4](03.system-pipeline-modules.md#1324-售卖机制):

```
RankScore = α × eCPM + β × OrgScore
```

Strategic interpretation of the two components:

- **eCPM (effective Cost Per Mille)**: derived from ad bids ([§1.4.3](#143-基础出价与智能出价)~[§1.4.5](#145-双出价)), reflecting the **commercial value** of the ad — how much the advertiser is willing to pay for this traffic
- **OrgScore (Organic Value Score)**: reflects the **platform's organic value** of the ad, combining ecosystem value (e.g., the advertised product's rating, shop service quality, user retention contribution) and content quality (e.g., title relevance, image quality, landing page experience)

Adjustments to the weight parameters **α / β** reflect the platform's strategic inclination at different stages:

- Emphasizing α → leaning toward revenue maximization
- Emphasizing β → leaning toward ecosystem health and user experience

> Blending is the **"final gatekeeper"** balancing revenue, ROI, and experience — it determines what users ultimately see and is the final landing point of all upstream optimizations in the advertising system. For details on blending implementations for each ad type, see Chapter 2 [§2.4.3](../02.ads-strategy/04.traffic-strategy-and-billing.md#243-混排和计费).

### 1.4.7 广告计费 / Ad Billing

#### 1.4.7.1 计费的定位 / The Role of Billing

<!-- last_translated: 2026-06-05T10:24:42.829007+00:00 -->

**Ad Billing (Ads Billing/Deduction)** is the step in the advertising system where advertisers are actually charged. In the ad pipeline described in [§1.3.1](03.system-pipeline-modules.md#131-系统链路全景), it is located in the **"Billing & Display"** phase; in the GMV MAX integrated pipeline, it corresponds to the Deduction module of API3.

Billing is directly related to the **"Billing Point"** concept in [§1.4.2](#142-广告出价和售卖的核心概念): when a user action triggers a billing event (e.g., click, impression), the system executes a deduction calculation and deducts the corresponding amount from the advertiser's budget.

#### 1.4.7.2 拍卖机制： GSP vs GFP / Auction Mechanisms: GSP vs. GFP

<!-- last_translated: 2026-06-05T10:24:47.075137+00:00 -->

The charged amount in an ad system is determined by the auction mechanism. The two mainstream auction mechanisms in the industry:

**GFP (Generalized First-Price)**: The auction winner pays at their own bid.

**GSP (Generalized Second-Price)**: The auction winner pays at the next position's price (plus a minimum increment δ).


| Dimension | GFP (Generalized First-Price) | GSP (Generalized Second-Price) |
| ------- | ----------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| Deduction Rule | Winner pays their own bid | Winner pays the next position's price + minimum increment δ |
| Advertiser Incentive | Encourages bid shading (bid < true value) | Encourages truthful bidding (bid ≈ true value) |
| Equilibrium Stability | Unstable; advertisers continuously probe for the minimum winning price | More stable; truthful bidding is a weakly dominant strategy |
| Platform Revenue Stability | High volatility; influenced by gaming behavior | More stable; deduction determined by competitive landscape |
| Industry Adoption | Some DSPs, programmatic display ads | Google Ads, Meta Ads, Shopee Paid Ads |


> **Shopee Paid Ads uses GSP (Generalized Second-Price) as the primary billing mechanism**, encouraging advertisers to bid truthfully, reducing gaming costs, and improving market efficiency.

#### 1.4.7.3 GSP 扣费公式推导 / GSP Deduction Formula Derivation

<!-- last_translated: 2026-06-05T10:24:58.214596+00:00 -->

Starting from the RankScore formula in [§1.3.2.4](03.system-pipeline-modules.md#1324-售卖机制), we derive the actual CPC deduction under GSP:

**Ranking formula:**

```
RankScore_i = α × eCPM_i + β × OrgScore_i
```

where `eCPM_i = Bid_i × pCTR_i` (under CPC bidding mode).

**GSP deduction condition:** The deduction for the i-th ranked ad is exactly the amount at which its RankScore equals the (i+1)-th position:

```
α × (actual_CPC_i × pCTR_i) + β × OrgScore_i = RankScore_{i+1}
```

**Solving for actual CPC:**

```
actual_CPC_i = (RankScore_{i+1} - β × OrgScore_i) / (α × pCTR_i) + δ
```

where **δ** is the minimum increment (typically 0.01), ensuring the winner's RankScore is strictly higher than the next position.

**Key Insights:**

- Under GSP, the actual deduction **depends only on the competitor's RankScore**, not on the winner's own bid — bidding higher does not lead to higher charges, it only improves the probability of winning
- Under oCPX smart bidding, the GSP deduction logic is unchanged — oCPX differences manifest at the upstream bidding stage ([§1.4.3](#143-基础出价与智能出价)~[§1.4.5](#145-双出价)); at the billing point level, standard GSP is still executed

#### 1.4.7.4 底价与扣费边界 / Reserve Price and Deduction Boundaries

<!-- last_translated: 2026-06-05T10:25:00.162061+00:00 -->

In real systems, GSP deductions also need to consider various boundary conditions:

> **Regarding Reserve Price:** The reserve price is an admission threshold at the **bidding/auction stage**, not a deduction floor at the billing stage. Ads with bids below the reserve price are directly excluded from auction ranking, thereby avoiding the inconsistency of "winning on rank at a low bid but being charged at the higher reserve price" — the price signals used for ranking and billing should be unified.

**Deduction Bounds (Min Cap / Max Cap):** Deduction boundaries are set based on the first-price (GFP, advertiser's bid) and second-price (GSP) respectively, to prevent extreme deductions:

- **Min Cap:** `min_cap = bid × gfp_min_ratio` (`gfp_min_ratio < 1`) — a discounted floor based on first-price; used as a safety net when GSP deduction is too low, protecting platform revenue
- **Max Cap:** `max_cap = min(GSP_price × gsp_max_ratio, bid)` (`gsp_max_ratio > 1`) — the lesser of an inflated second-price and the first-price. Compared to using only `bid` as a ceiling, additionally introducing `GSP_price × gsp_max_ratio` prevents extreme deductions caused by model estimation anomalies (e.g., an abnormally high pCTR estimate leading to an abnormally high `bid`)

Design rationale: use the **first-price (advertiser's bid) to control the floor** and use **second-price (GSP deduction) + first-price as a dual ceiling** — the floor is anchored to the advertiser's willingness to pay; the ceiling is simultaneously anchored to both the competitive landscape and the willingness to pay. Both ends prevent "deduction too low hurting the platform" and "deduction too high hurting the advertiser."

**Final deduction formula:**

```
min_cap = bid × gfp_min_ratio                   // First-price safety net (gfp_min_ratio < 1)
max_cap = min(GSP_price × gsp_max_ratio, bid)   // Second-price ceiling + first-price hard cap (gsp_max_ratio > 1)
actual_charge = clip(GSP_price, min_cap, max_cap)
```

where `clip(x, lo, hi) = max(lo, min(x, hi))`.

- When competition is sufficient, `GSP_price` falls within `[min_cap, max_cap]`; actual charge = standard GSP second-price
- When competition is insufficient and GSP deduction is too low, actual charge is floored by `min_cap`, equivalent to a discounted first-price
- When competition is intense and GSP deduction is high, actual charge is capped by `max_cap` — primarily constrained by `GSP_price × gsp_max_ratio`, with `bid` as the ultimate hard ceiling in extreme cases
- When model estimation is abnormal and `bid` is abnormally high, `GSP_price × gsp_max_ratio` is still based on real competitive second-price and does not follow `bid` upward, effectively preventing extreme deductions

#### 1.4.7.5 智能出价下的计费特殊性 / Special Characteristics of Billing Under Smart Bidding

<!-- last_translated: 2026-06-05T10:25:17.188340+00:00 -->

Under oCPX smart bidding ([§1.4.3.2](#1432-智能出价-smart-bidding-ocpx)), billing has some characteristics that differ from manual bidding:

**High per-charge volatility**: oCPX dynamically adjusts bids via the λ repricing coefficient ([§1.4.3.3](#1433-λ-调价系数)), leading to significant differences in per-click CPC charges across different traffic. Advertisers should measure performance by **aggregate CPA/ROAS** rather than per-click CPC.

**Cost protection and compensation mechanism**: The platform offers a cost protection commitment to oCPX advertisers — during the cold start learning period, if the average CPA exceeds the target value, the platform will compensate for the excess:

```
compensation = actual_cost - target_CPA × conversions  (compensation is triggered if the result is positive)
```


| Dimension | Manual Bidding (Manual CPC) | Smart Bidding (oCPX) |
| ------ | ---------------- | ------------------- |
| Per-charge behavior | Stable; deviation from bid is predictable | Highly volatile; influenced by dynamic λ adjustments |
| Cost control mechanism | Advertisers directly control through bids | Platform automatically controls aggregate cost via λ algorithm |
| Risk bearer | Advertiser (bid = maximum cost) | Platform (commits to Target CPA; provides compensation guarantee) |
| Performance measurement granularity | Per-click cost | Aggregate conversion cost (CPA / ROAS) |


**Q: In the era of smart bidding, what are the respective advantages and disadvantages of GSP (second-price) vs. GFP (first-price) billing?**

[§1.4.7.2](#1472-拍卖机制-gsp-vs-gfp) compared GSP and GFP from a traditional perspective. However, in the era of smart bidding (oCPX) + unified blending ([§1.4.6.2](#1462-独立链路-vs-融合链路)), two classic advantages of GSP are weakened: ① Under oCPX, bids are generated by the platform's λ algorithm ([§1.4.3.3](#1433-λ-调价系数)); advertisers don't directly control bids, so GSP's "encourages truthful bidding" loses its target; ② Under unified blending, suppressing bids → eCPM drops → RankScore falls → ability to win traffic significantly declines, and this mechanism itself has already replaced the anti-bid-shading function of GSP — this holds regardless of whether GSP or GFP is used.


| Dimension | GSP (Generalized Second-Price) | GFP (Generalized First-Price) |
| ---------- | ------------------------------------------ | ---------------------- |
| Incentive significance under smart bidding | Weakened — bids are generated by platform λ; truthful bidding incentive loses its target | Unaffected — first-price needs no incentive mechanism |
| Anti-bid-shading capability | Traditional advantage, but gap narrows under unified blending | Relies on natural suppression by unified blending + oCPX platform pricing |
| Advertiser cost perception | More friendly (deduction < bid, varies with competition level) | Higher (deduction = bid), may suppress willingness to bid |
| Revenue when competition is insufficient | Loss; requires min_cap safety net ([§1.4.7.4](#1474-底价与扣费边界)) | No loss; bid = deduction |
| Platform cost protection pressure | Lower (second-price is naturally lower than first-price) | Higher (first-price charges more; increased compensation risk) |
| System complexity | High (second-price formula + min/max cap + next position's RankScore) | Low (bid = deduction) |
| Industry trend | Google/Meta gradually shifting to GFP as smart bidding share increases | ← |


> **Summary**: In the era of smart bidding + unified blending, the classic advantages of GSP (incentivizing truthful bidding, preventing bid shading) are significantly weakened. GFP is becoming an increasingly attractive choice due to the simplicity of its deduction logic, but GSP still holds advantages in advertiser cost perception and platform compensation pressure. Migration decisions must consider the business stage and advertiser ecosystem comprehensively. Industry evolution: Google Ads switched search ads from GSP to GFP in 2019; Meta Ads followed suit, applying GFP to auto-bid ads; Amazon Ads shifted to GFP in 2021; domestically, ByteDance (Juhao Engine) adopted GFP relatively early, while Baidu Phoenix Nest and Tencent Ads continue to use GSP primarily.

#### 1.4.7.6 反作弊与计费修正 / Anti-Fraud and Billing Correction

<!-- last_translated: 2026-06-05T10:24:58.173450+00:00 -->

Ad billing must work with the **Anti-Fraud System** to filter Invalid Traffic (IVT), ensuring advertisers only pay for genuine and effective user actions:

**Real-time Filtering (Pre-Billing)**: Before billing, identify and filter invalid traffic in real time based on click patterns, time intervals, device fingerprints, IP characteristics, etc. Filtered clicks/impressions do not trigger charges.

**Post-hoc Correction (Post-Billing)**: Through offline retrospective analysis, identify invalid traffic that slipped through the real-time stage, and issue refunds for already-charged amounts.

| Dimension | Real-time Filtering (Pre-Billing) | Post-hoc Correction (Post-Billing) |
| ---- | ---------------------- | ----------------------- |
| Execution Timing | Before billing (millisecond-level) | After billing (hours/days-level) |
| Detection Method | Rule engine + real-time model (click frequency, device fingerprint) | Offline model + big data analysis (behavioral sequence, conversion funnel) |
| Handling Result | Filter invalid clicks; no charge triggered | Identify invalid charges; execute refunds |
| Coverage | High (covers most simple fraud) | Supplemental (captures complex/hidden fraud) |

> System implementation of anti-fraud and billing correction is covered in Chapter 3 [§3.6](../03.ads-engine/06.ads-data.md#36-广告数据ads-data) (Ads Data).
